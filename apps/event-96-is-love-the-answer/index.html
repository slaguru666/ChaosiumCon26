Session Brief
One-minute brief — read before play begins
You are the landing crew of the Foolish Endeavour, dispatched to conduct a cultural survey of Wheel-Spoke — a circular trading settlement built around the base of an ancient Ringworld spire. The mission should take three hours. It will not.

Something in this settlement has been waiting for you. You don't know it yet.
⬡ THE BOND MECHANIC
Watch the table in Act One. The player who engages most directly with Serenthis becomes the bond character. Default: Dr. Mira Chen. The bond is the emotional centre of this scenario — everything else is scaffolding. Do not rush it.

~15 minutes · 0:00 → 0:15
Atmosphere set · Bond character identified · Mission briefed
The landing craft sets down two hundred metres outside the settlement boundary, in a flat clearing that your instruments say has been used as a landing point before. How long ago is harder to determine.

Wheel-Spoke sits at the base of a spire that rises — you cannot see the top of it — into the upper atmosphere. The settlement is exactly what its name implies: circular, with eight radial avenues running from a central plaza out to a ring-road market. It is old. The scrith foundation beneath the buildings is older still. What the inhabitants have built on top of it is vivid and dense and loud.

Your mission is a cultural survey. Standard procedure: observe, document, do not destabilise. You have six hours before the Foolish Endeavour must reposition.

Vorn's voice comes through the comms: "Weather systems are doing something I don't like. Not a problem yet. Keep the mission short."

The market gates are open.
GM Note — The BondIdentify the bond character before Act One begins. Watch who engages most openly with the setting. The bond character is whoever treats Wheel-Spoke as a place rather than an obstacle. Default is Mira Chen. Flex accordingly.

~60 minutes · 0:15 → 1:15
Goal: Serenthis met · Bond formed · Ghresh-Ka threat established
ACT ONE JOB: Make Serenthis real before the danger arrives. Give each player one market moment. The bond should form through play, not instruction. The collapse trap triggers at 1:00 latest if players haven't found her naturally.
The Market Ring▼
Outer ring of Wheel-Spoke: continuous market, stalls and awnings, traders of a dozen hominid species. Bright fabric, bartered goods, four languages at once. The central plaza and spire are visible through gaps. Warm, chaotic, alive. Give each player a market moment — an odd item, an unusual trader, a strange custom. This is the only pure joy the scenario provides.

At the base of one radial avenue: a collapsed awning has exposed old scrith foundations with a maintenance hatch that's been covered by market stalls. Browsing one stall causes the owner to shift position — depressing a contact panel in the wall. This is the collapse trap.
[Ringworld: Awareness — spot instability before panel triggers. Hard. If failed, trap fires.]
The Collapse Trap → Serenthis▼
Wall panel triggers scrith reconfiguration. Two stalls drop half a metre, roof collapses. 1D6 damage, player pinned. Crowd scatters in a circle.

Before any crew member reaches the trapped player — Serenthis is already there. She crosses the crowd in seconds, places both hands on the panel in the exposed wall. The scrith lifts. She does not appear surprised. She appears to have been expecting this moment.
[First Aid — reduce damage to 1 if treated within 1 minute]
This is the first meeting. The bond character is the one Serenthis looks at last. She holds their eye for a moment longer than necessary before looking away.
Serenthis's Workshop▼
Narrow building on the inner ring. Floor-to-ceiling samples, weaving tools, maps, small artefacts. Cot in the back. Cooking fire. She brings the crew here to treat the injured player and to look at them properly.

Key items: Maps on the walls — far more detailed than any survey should produce; one shows a chamber beneath the spire not in any public record. A flat oval of unknown material (dormant Rememberer interface token) grows warm when the bond character touches it. Woven bands on Serenthis's wrists are an encoded lineage record.
[Awareness — map shows vault chamber beneath spire]
[Touch — interface token responds to bond character only; Serenthis says she's never seen it react before]
Scene NoteThis scene should breathe. Don't rush to the threat. Let the bond character talk to her. Let them ask questions. She answers directly. She does not perform mysteriousness.
Act One PacingAt 1:00 — trigger the collapse trap if it hasn't fired. Act One ends when Woven-Branch arrives at the workshop and says: "Ghresh-Ka will be here by tomorrow's shadow cycle. And the vault will not wait that long."

Goal: Vault truth revealed · Ghresh-Ka forces arrive · Storm clock critical
Three Simultaneous ThreadsVault expedition (main thread) · Ghresh-Ka's advance force (escalating threat) · Ship comms tightening clock (pressure). Players will feel pulled in multiple directions. This is intentional.
The Spire Base Passage▼
200m descent at gentle angle. Scrith walls. Dim bioluminescent growth. The air is different — cooler, cleaner, smelling of something unnameable.

Partway down: a scrith iris sealed for centuries. Serenthis places her hand on the wall beside it. The iris opens. She is visibly shaken by the ease of it. First time she's done this consciously.
[Awareness — iris shows signs of recent opening, 3–4 times in last decade]
The Rememberer's Vault▼
40m diameter, 20m high dome. Passive lighting activates fully when Serenthis enters. Walls covered in organic interface panels. Central dais with a 2m column covered in notation no one recognises.

The column notation is pre-Pak Protector-script. Full translation: this is an emergency uplift mechanism — if Ringworld maintenance fails critically, a Rememberer accepts the full encoding and becomes a distributed Protector-class intelligence capable of emergency repair. The transition is irreversible.
[Computer + Linguistics Hard — partial translation]
[Science (Physics) + Computer Extreme — full understanding of encoding mechanism]
Fallback: If no one achieves the roll, Serenthis translates it herself — haltingly, like reading her own name in a language she learned this morning. She understands what it says. She tells them.

Goal: The choice. 30 minutes. It always happens.
The SprintVault to landing craft: 3 miles. Storm: 40 minutes from Wheel-Spoke. Ship: under 1 hour before forced reposition. Ghresh-Ka: behind them.
[DEX × 5 — maintain pace over rough terrain]
[CON × 5 — sustain pace if already injured]
The Landing Craft Clearing▼
Open. Final. No cover. Storm wall visible to the north. Ghresh-Ka's force visible to the east.

Math: Landing craft lifts in 4 minutes from standby. Ghresh-Ka is 8 minutes away. Storm is 15 minutes from this position. There is time — but only if the crew stops doing anything else.
The Interception▼
Ghresh-Ka comes himself, with two guards. Calls a halt at 200m. Speaks calmly:

"I am not interested in your crew. I am not interested in your ship. I am interested in one person. Give her to me and you leave. We do not need to lose anyone today."

This is a genuine offer. He means it. He'd rather not fight a crew with a Known Space vessel in orbit.

RR'raan option: Ghresh-Ka will accept a one-on-one honour challenge, pausing his force. This buys time and may end the threat entirely.
[Combat: Ghresh-Ka HP 18, Armour 3, DB +1D6, Claws 85%, Carbine 70%]
⬡ The Moral Dilemma
Serenthis speaks to the bond character. The crew can hear. She says: "What do you want me to do?"
Option A — Come With Us
Serenthis boards the craft. She leaves the Ringworld, her people, her settlement, everything she has ever known. The encoding is incomplete — she will live with it unresolved. Ghresh-Ka is left without his key. Woven-Branch without her Rememberer.
Is love taking someone from their world because you cannot bear to leave them?
Option B — She Stays
The crew leaves. They cannot fight Ghresh-Ka, reach the ship, and protect Serenthis simultaneously. She remains with her people. She has partial vault access. Maybe she wins. Maybe she doesn't. The bond character will never know.
Is love letting go when holding on would break what you love?
Option C — She Transforms (vault accessed in Act Two)
Serenthis calls what she is becoming. The storm responds. Shadow squares shift. Ghresh-Ka's force is dispersed with impossible precision. She is alive. But she is already different. The person the bond character knew is becoming something the Ringworld built her to be.
Is love enough when the person you love is becoming something greater than the love you had?
Option D — The Crew Stays and Fights
They miss the ship. They fight Ghresh-Ka. They may win. The ship comes back in six hours. Six hours on the Ringworld surface in the aftermath of a storm. Serenthis alive and unchanged. The bond character chose her over the mission.
Is love worth breaking every rule you came with?
At 3:10Ghresh-Ka's terms expire — force resumes advance. This forces a decision.
At 3:15 — Hard CloseStorm front reaches landing zone. Craft must lift NOW or be grounded for hours. Ask the bond character directly: "What do you say to her?" Do not let the dilemma drift. Last five minutes.
⏱
~15 minutes · 3:15 → 3:30
Play the outcome. Close the session.
Option A — Taken
Serenthis boards the craft
Off the Ringworld for the first time. She sits by the viewport for the entire ascent, not speaking. The encoding is unresolved. What happens to her is a campaign question.
Option B — Left Behind
Crew departs without Serenthis
Clean departure. The bond character watches the surface recede. Woven-Branch's people were grouping at the vault entrance as they lifted. Whether Serenthis survives the next confrontation with Ghresh-Ka is genuinely unknown.
Option C — Transformed
Vault interface completed, encoding full
Crew lifts on schedule. Serenthis stands at the vault entrance as they go. She raises a hand. The gesture is hers. The thing behind the eyes that makes it her gesture may not be. The storm dissipates thirty minutes after departure.
Option D — Stayed
Crew misses the ship
Ghresh-Ka defeated or retreated. Six hours on the surface. Serenthis alive. The bond character sits next to her while they wait. When the Foolish Endeavour returns, Vorn's first words: "I'm filing the incident report. You're explaining it."
Closing — Option A (read aloud):

The landing craft clears the storm front three minutes after lift.
Serenthis has not moved from the viewport.
She says, without turning: "I could feel the vault from here. Until now."
A pause. "I can't feel it anymore."
Another pause. Longer.
"Is that what leaving always feels like?"

The bond character may answer. There is no right answer. The session ends.
Serenthis
Ringworld Hominid · Rememberer Lineage
⬡ Bond
Appearance
Female, mid-twenties. Tall for the settlement population. Dark skin, close-cut hair with a single braid from temple to shoulder. Layered fabrics in greys and deep greens. Woven bands at both wrists — lineage record. Eyes that listen like they're taking notes.
Motivation
She doesn't yet understand why these off-worlders matter, but she's known for three days they were coming. Her dreams told her. She wants to understand what she is, and believes these people may be the only ones who can help.
What She Knows
The vault beneath the spire exists — she feels it. She can interface with Ringworld systems (no language for it until now). Ghresh-Ka has been sending scouts for two seasons. The elders are frightened of what she is becoming.

Ghresh-Ka
Kzin Warlord · Ringworld Territorial Commander
⚠ Threat
Appearance
Nearly 3 metres, heavily muscled. Orange-cream fur, short at muzzle, longer mane. Adapted Ringworld battle-mesh over Kzin undersuit — functional, battered, used. One ear half-missing. Amber eyes, always calculating. He does not snarl. He does not posture. He watches.
Motivation
Vault access via the Rememberer key. Eleven years building a territorial base for this. He wants the vault's systems to function as weapons or leverage at Known Space political scale. He is not wrong that the vault is powerful. He is catastrophically wrong about what the encoding does to the key.
What He Knows
What a Rememberer is, broadly. That the encoding transition happens — he does not know what it produces. He believes the resulting entity will be controllable. It will not.
