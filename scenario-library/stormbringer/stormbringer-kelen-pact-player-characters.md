# The Storm of Kelen's Pact — Player Character Sheets
*Stormbringer 1st Edition — Six Pre-Generated Characters*

---

> **For the GM:** These six characters are designed to give each player a distinct role and tone at the table.
> Davin and Brenn anchor the physical reality of the scenario. Ysolde and Sarath carry the sorcerous weight.
> Cray carries the personal grief. Lian carries the moral question. Together they cover every mechanical
> and dramatic angle the scenario requires. Hand them out face down. Let players choose by archetype
> description — not by stats.

## Demon-Bound Item: *"Cold Comfort"*

**Type:** Demon of Combat, bound in the crossguard of Davin's broadsword
**Demon Characteristics:** STR 13 | POW 12 | INT 5
**Bound by:** A Pan Tang weaponsmith. Davin won the blade in a dice game three years ago.

**What it does:**
- The blade adds **+1D4 to damage** on any attack roll that succeeds by 20% or more (i.e., rolled 20 or more below skill)
- **Once per combat:** The demon can deflect a single successful hit against Davin — the blade twitches in its scabbard and the blow deflects without a parry roll. The attacker must re-roll their attack. Davin feels a cold shock up his arm when this occurs.
- The crossguard is cold to the touch when living enemies are within ten yards.

**Cost:** The demon feeds on Davin's small supply of luck. After using the deflection ability, Davin suffers −10% to all non-combat skill rolls for the remainder of the scene.

**What Davin knows:** That the sword is "lucky." That it gets cold near trouble. That he has never dropped it in a fight, which he attributes to good grip and good nerves.
**What Davin does not know:** That the demon is slowly learning Davin's fighting style and will, in time, begin anticipating moves Davin hasn't made yet. This is not relevant to this scenario. It is worth mentioning to the player at the end.

---

## Background

Davin Kell served ten years in a Lormyrian mercenary company before the company was disbanded after a catastrophic contract dispute with a Duke who turned out to be dead. He has spent the last five years freelancing across the Eastern Marches, taking work that pays and leaving before he gets too comfortable.

He is not complicated. He is good at fighting and has decided that being good at fighting is enough of a life. He is fair in negotiation, reliable in a crisis, and has approximately zero interest in questions of Law, Chaos, or the Cosmic Balance. He has seen men argue about those things in taverns and watched them die about them on the road. He is not curious about which is correct.

Six months ago, the sorcerer Kelen hired him to escort supplies to the moor treeline. He was told to wait, given half his fee, and never saw Kelen again. He took the current job partly for the remaining four silver. Partly because something is wrong out there and he was part of getting it started, which makes it his business whether he likes it or not.

**At the table:** Davin is the character who notices things other characters overlook (high Spot, moderate Insight). He provides competent violence when needed. He is the practical anchor when the sorcerous elements of the scenario get strange. Play him as steady, slightly tired, and quietly observant.

**Quote:** *"I'll need the rest of the silver when it's done. That was the arrangement."*

## Demon-Bound Item: *"The Copper Mirror"*

**Type:** Demon of Knowledge (Intellect Demon), bound in a small hand mirror
**Demon Characteristics:** INT 16 | POW 10 | CHA 8
**Bound by:** A Vilmirian scholar-sorcerer — Ysolde's own mentor, three years ago, as a research tool.

**What it does:**
- **Once per session:** Ysolde holds the mirror and asks one question. The demon answers truthfully about: Melnibonéan history, demon lore, magical theory, the nature of entities she has directly observed. Answers are accurate but may be incomplete.
- **The catch:** The demon always tells her one additional truth she was not asking about. This second truth is chosen by the GM and is always relevant to the scenario — often uncomfortably so.
- The reflection in the mirror sometimes shows scenes from the past rather than the present. This is not controllable. Ysolde has learned to pay attention when it happens.
- **Passive:** When Ysolde consults the mirror actively (holds it and concentrates), any Lore roll she makes in the same scene is at +15%.

**Cost:** No POW cost. However, the demon grows curious about events and occasionally shows Ysolde things she did not ask to see — including things happening nearby that she cannot otherwise observe. This is the GM's tool, not a player one.

**What Ysolde knows:** Everything. She bound it herself (under supervision). She is aware of all its abilities. She treats it as a research instrument and has given it a name she does not share: Pellucid.

---

## Background

Ysolde's family are minor Vilmirian nobility — legal administrators and record-keepers. She was educated at a private school, showed exceptional aptitude for languages and historical analysis, and was recruited into the academic sorcery tradition at nineteen. Her specialism is Melnibonéan material culture and the legacy of the Dragon Isle's administration in the Young Kingdoms.

She has been researching the Spire of Kyelm for three years via documents, oral history, and two previous field visits to the moor — neither of which permitted entry to the Spire itself. This current situation, which everyone else finds alarming, is professionally extraordinary. She has already filled four pages of notes since arriving in Mael's Ford this morning.

She is not unaware of the danger. She is choosing to treat documentation as a coping mechanism. She is terrified. She will not admit this.

**At the table:** Ysolde is the character who knows what things are. She is the exposition engine, but should not be allowed to info-dump — the GM should call for rolls on things she doesn't automatically know, to keep the discoveries feeling earned. She has the highest POW of any character, which will matter for binding/summoning in Act Three.

**Quote:** *"I need you to describe exactly what you saw. Every detail. Don't edit for impact — just tell me what happened."*

## Demon-Bound Item: *"The Black Pins"*

**Type:** Demon of Stealth, bound across a set of five iron hairpins
**Demon Characteristics:** DEX 15 | INT 9 | POW 8
**Bound by:** Unknown — she took these from a dead woman in Nadsokor who had no further use for them.

**What it does:**
- **Passive:** While wearing the pins (worked into her hair), Cray's Stealth skill is +20% (to 90%, which is the effective cap for the scenario).
- **Once per session:** Cray can move in complete silence for up to ten combat rounds regardless of surface. No Stealth roll required during this time. She produces no sound — no footsteps, no cloth rustle, no breathing sounds from close range.
- The pins feel slightly warm when someone nearby is trying to find her.

**Cost:** The demon is not entirely benign. While the pins are worn, Cray occasionally hears one quiet word — a name, a number, a fragment of a sentence — that means nothing in context but is always something the dead woman knew. This has been happening for six months. She has not connected it to the pins.

**What Cray knows:** That the hairpins seem to help her move quietly. She has used them for six months without understanding why they work. She does not know they are demon-bound. She would be unsettled if told.

**What Cray does not know:** The dead woman's name was Sevel's mother. This is not necessary to the scenario. It is purely a narrative gift if the GM wants to use it.

---

## Background

Cray grew up in Nadsokor — the Beggar Kingdom — which means she grew up in a city that the rest of the Young Kingdoms prefers to pretend does not exist. She left at seventeen, has been moving ever since, and has developed a habit of knowing where the exits are before she needs them.

She is good at two things: getting into places she is not supposed to be, and knowing when people are lying. She works as a freelance information broker, courier, and occasional retrieval specialist. She does not call herself a thief. The distinction matters to her.

Three months ago, Sevel — Tally's older brother, and someone Cray knew from a previous job — went missing near the Eastern Moors. Nobody connected this to Kelen. Cray did, eventually. She is here partly for the coin, primarily for the clue. She will not say this to anyone and will deflect if directly asked.

**At the table:** Cray is the character with the personal stake. The GM should give her player private moments — the cloak fabric in the Binding Hall (Act Two), the Sevel information from Tally (Act One). She is emotionally sealed and will come across as mercenary until the private hook lands. She is the fastest character (highest Strike Rank) and extremely competent in non-combat situations.

**Quote:** *"I'm not here for reasons. I'm here for the money. Can we move on?"*

## Demon-Bound Item: *"The Chain of Regret"*

**Type:** Demon of Warding (a Guardian Demon of unusual alignment — not Chaos but Balance)
**Demon Characteristics:** CON 14 | POW 13 | STR 10
**Bound by:** A Balance-aligned sorcerer in Tanelorn, six years ago. Given to Sarath as payment for information.

**What it does:**

**What Sarath knows:** Everything. He is a former cultist with genuine sorcerous knowledge. He knows what it is, how it works, and what it can do. He has made peace with it. He has not made peace with the fact that a Balance-aligned demon chose to bind itself to him, which implies something about what the Balance thinks of his future.

---

## Background

Sarath was raised in the outer territories of Pan Tang — not the Tiger Isle itself but the administered territories — and joined the Cult of Xiombarg at twenty-three because in Pan Tang the question is not whether you serve Chaos but which face of it you choose. He served for eleven years. He was good at it.

The cult-master performed a binding that went wrong. The demon did not unmake the man quickly. Sarath watched for four hours. He left that evening, taking nothing except his scimitar and the memory of those four hours, which have not left him since.

He has spent fifteen years trying to be on the right side of things. He is not certain there is a right side. He thinks there might be a less catastrophic side, which is where Balance comes in. He reads the pattern of Mael's Ford's storms the way a physician reads symptoms, and he does not like what he is reading.

**At the table:** Sarath is the character who knows what Vorak is and what the ritual requires. He is the one other characters come to when they need an expert on Chaos — which is uncomfortable for everyone, including him. He is the character the GM should use for the most morally weighted moments. His binding scroll in Act Three is a genuine dilemma: he has it; using it will cost him POW; he does not want to, and he has reasons. Let the player arrive at the decision themselves.

**Quote:** *"I'm not going to tell you what I've done. I'll tell you what I know. They're not the same thing."*

## Demon-Bound Item: *"The True Compass"*

**Type:** Demon of Seeking (Intellect Demon), bound in a brass nautical compass
**Demon Characteristics:** INT 13 | POW 11 | DEX 9
**Bound by:** A Tarkeshite port-sorcerer, as a navigation aid for long-haul merchant captains. Standard trade item on the southern sea routes.

**What it does:**
- **Navigation (standard function):** The compass always points true north and is accurate within the Resistance Table even through magical interference or underground. No roll required for standard navigation.
- **Seeking (unusual function):** If Brenn holds the compass and concentrates for one full uninterrupted round, it points toward whatever or whatever he most wants to find — not what he asks for, what he wants. The distinction matters. The demon reads intent, not instruction.
- The seeking function has a range of approximately one mile in the open, less through stone.

**Cost:** The "most wants" mechanic is the GM's to interpret. What Brenn most wants to find is not always what he thinks it is, and the compass is honest about this. Brenn found this out two years ago when the compass pointed toward the sea rather than his crewmates after the ship went down. He has not entirely forgiven it.

**What Brenn knows:** Everything about the navigation function. He suspects the seeking function works on a principle he does not fully understand. He uses it anyway.

---

## Background

Brenn captained a Tarkeshite merchant vessel for twelve years — mid-sized, honest work, decent margins. The ship sank two years ago in a storm that had no reason to be where it was. He got four crew members out of the water. He left twelve behind.

He is not broken by this. He is changed by it. He does not talk about it unless directly asked, and then he answers briefly and accurately and moves on. He has been working short-haul river contracts since, which pay less and require less of him. He docked at the nearest river port, heard about a job in Mael's Ford, and took it because the money is reasonable and because walking away from work that needs doing is not something he does.

He has already decided this situation is worse than advertised. He is going to stay until it resolves because someone is going to need to carry a body — or an injured person — out of wherever this ends, and he is the most physically capable person in this group. He knows this and has accepted it without being asked.

**At the table:** Brenn is competent, reliable, and self-aware. He is the character who keeps the group functional when things go bad — highest HP in the group, good at physical tasks, steady under pressure. He provides wry, practical commentary that grounds the scene. He is not here for the moral question but will engage with it honestly when it arrives.

**Quote:** *"Tell me where we're going. I'll figure out the rest when we get there."*

## Demon-Bound Item: *"The Law's Lantern"*

**Type:** Demon of Order (a rare subtype — not Chaos-aligned, but an elemental of structure and pattern)
**Demon Characteristics:** POW 14 | INT 12 | CON 11
**Bound by:** A Law-aligned sorcerer of the Ilmioran Church, as a field instrument for investigators dealing with Chaos incursions.

**What it does:**
- **Passive:** The lantern never goes out in wind, rain, or magical darkness. Its flame cannot be extinguished by natural means.
- **Revelation:** The lantern's light causes Chaos-tainted entities and objects to emit a faint blue luminescence visible to all observers within the lit area. Highly Chaos-attuned beings (POW 16+) may resist with a POW vs POW 14 roll.
- **Warding:** Any Chaos entity attempting to directly approach Lian must make a POW vs POW 14 roll or feel strong reluctance (they can still act, but approach costs them an action as they resist the ward). This does not apply to human Chaos-worshippers — only to entities with Chaos as their fundamental nature.

**Cost:** The demon is not without opinion. When Lian does something the demon considers contrary to Law — which it defines more rigidly than Lian does — the lantern dims briefly. This is a visible signal to everyone nearby. It has happened twice so far on this journey. Lian is not sure the others noticed.

**What Lian knows:** The lantern was issued by the Church. Lian understands its general function. The Church describes it as "Law-aligned" and is deliberately vague about the mechanism. Lian has been told it contains a "bound principle of order" and that this is not the same as Chaos sorcery. They have accepted this.

**What Lian has not resolved:** Whether a demon bound for Law's purposes is still a demon. The Church's answer is that it is not. Lian suspects the Church's answer may be convenient. This is the beginning of Lian's actual theological crisis, which the scenario's final act will accelerate.

---

## Background

Lian was born to a minor Ilmioran merchant family, educated in a Church of Law school, and recruited into the investigative branch of the Church at twenty-one after demonstrating exceptional Insight scores and a genuine commitment to Law's principles — not the institutional kind, the actual kind.

This is their first real field assignment. They have read extensively. They are prepared. They are not prepared.

The gap between "Chaos is a destructive force that threatens the Young Kingdoms" (as stated in the training texts) and what Chaos actually looks like, smells like, and does to people and places — this gap is what Act Two is going to teach Lian. By Act Three, Lian will face a choice where every option compromises Law's principles in some way. The scenario's moral weight is primarily carried by this character.

Lian is not naive. They are inexperienced, which is different. They ask too many earnest questions. They take notes. They take the work seriously because they believe the work matters, which is both their greatest quality and the source of their worst moments tonight.

**At the table:** Give the Lian player moments. Act Three is designed around their moral crisis — the Church's answer was always clean, and clean answers don't survive contact with the Spire of Kyelm. The Divine Intervention is there as an emergency exit that comes at real cost. Do not make it easy. Do not make it impossible.

**Quote:** *"Law's mercy. I didn't think it would — I mean, I knew it would be difficult. I prepared for difficult. This is something else."*

### Allegiance Tensions
Sarath has both the highest Chaos score and the highest Balance score at the table. He has seen what Chaos costs. He has not stopped understanding it. This is not contradiction — it is experience. The player should know both numbers.

Lian has a Law score of 18 — the highest alignment score in the group. The scenario's three resolutions all compromise Law in some way. This is intentional.

### Demon Items in Play
All six demon items are low-power and narrative. None of them resolve the scenario's central problem. They add texture and individual character — they are not trump cards.

The GM should note: Ysolde's mirror can provide one piece of information per session from the GM. Plan what that information is in advance. The "second truth the demon tells" should be something that recontextualises what the players already know — not new exposition, but a new angle on existing evidence.
