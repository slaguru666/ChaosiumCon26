## Premise

The Operatives receive a Blue BPN to investigate and secure the abandoned Keller-Voss Energy Generation Plant in Downtown District 2. Power fluctuations and civilian disappearances in the surrounding blocks have escalated the classification from routine maintenance to medium-high threat — partly because the previous squad sent to investigate, Hammer-6, went in six days ago and didn't come back. One survivor was pulled out screaming. The media has been D-Noticed. Something is very wrong inside that building, and SLA Industries wants it handled quietly, quickly, and without awkward questions about what was really happening in that plant.

---

## Background

*(For GM use only — see GM Companion for full detail)*

The Keller-Voss plant was decommissioned 14 months ago — officially. Unofficially, a rogue team from the Department of Research continued clandestine experiments inside: attempting to siphon energy directly from the Ebb/White Earth interface, using technology developed during the Conflict Wars in partnership with Dark Lament. Three weeks ago, the containment chamber ruptured. Something crossed over from the spaces between dimensions. The researchers called it **The Hollow Current**. It feeds on bioelectric energy — life force — and has been growing in intelligence ever since, absorbing the minds of everyone it kills. The lead researchers didn't die. They were subsumed, their personalities folded into the entity's gestalt consciousness. They now serve as thralls, luring new victims deeper into the facility. SLA Industries is aware a disaster has occurred. Internal Affairs is already on site managing the cover-up. The Operatives are being sent to fix the problem — but nobody above them wants the truth to surface.

## Structure

### Scene 1 — The Briefing and the Red Flags

The Operatives receive the BPN through normal channels. Standard Blue maintenance work — except the threat rating has been revised upward, the previous squad is largely dead, and there's a D-Notice in place. Experienced Operatives will know that Blue BPNs don't come with D-Notices. This is the moment to ask questions, do background research, and decide how to approach the mission.

**What they can find out before entering:**

- Hammer-6's casualty report is available but heavily redacted. Only the survivor's name (Operative Markus Trell, SCL 7) and location (SLA Medical Facility 18-North, Room 447) are legible.
- The Keller-Voss plant was officially a standard power generation facility. Its decommissioning paperwork is clean and complete. Too clean — no anomalies, no lingering maintenance issues. Decommissioning reports for plants this old are never this tidy.
- Twelve civilians have disappeared in three weeks from Blocks 47–52. Shiver reports classify all disappearances as gang activity (Volt Runners, a local Sector 18 crew). This is obviously inadequate given the scale.
- The Volt Runners themselves have graffitied the perimeter fence with "STAY OUT - THEY'RE WATCHING." This is not typical gang territorial behaviour.

**Investigation options:**

- Visit Operative Trell at SLA Medical: he's lucid between 0800–1000 and wants to talk to someone who'll believe him.
- Contact Shiver Sergeant Kade at Sector 18 command: 20-year veteran, knows something is wrong, has incomplete blueprints to share.
- Visit the site externally before committing to entry: surveillance, reconnaissance, talking to locals near the fence.

---

### Scene 2 — The Survivor

SLA Medical Facility 18-North, Room 447. Trell is gaunt, haunted, and refuses to sleep without the lights on. Brain scans show unusual electrical activity — something the medical staff attribute to trauma but cannot fully explain.

He will talk, but his testimony comes in fragments. He's not lying. He's filtering trauma.

**What Trell tells them:**

- The plant was doing something wrong. Not just storing power — something else entirely.
- They followed emergency distress beacons down into the lower levels. The beacons led them to it.
- The electrical systems can't be trusted. It lives in them. It speaks through them.
- The scientists are still down there, but they're not people anymore. They're part of it.
- His squad died drained — like something extracted the life out of them directly. He ran while they were still screaming.
- Don't trust the lights. Don't trust the security feeds. Don't trust any voice that comes from a speaker.

If the Operatives earn his trust (patience, genuine concern, not pushing too hard), he will reveal the data chip hidden in his boot — partial security footage showing Dr. Vasquez deliberately initiating the final experiment against explicit safety protocols. This is crucial evidence if the Operatives plan to expose what happened.

**Complication:** A nurse at this facility is a source for Third Cycle News reporter Lyssa Tam. Tam may be attempting to reach Trell at the same time. If the Operatives arrive during visiting hours, they may encounter her — or find she's already been turned away by security.

---

### Scene 3 — Kade and the Perimeter

Shiver Sergeant Elias Kade controls the perimeter around Keller-Voss. He's a 20-year veteran who knows this isn't gang activity and has been ordered to say it is. He can't go in. He won't send his people in. That's why the BPN exists.

**What Kade provides:**

- Incomplete facility blueprints (ground level and Sub-Level 1 only — the experimental wing was built off-books and doesn't appear anywhere official).
- Background on the twelve missing civilians — names, last known locations, the pattern that clearly centres on the plant.
- His personal notes: dates and identification numbers of late-night supply trucks he observed making deliveries to the plant during its supposed decommissioning period. These trucks came from Department of Research logistics depots.
- His observation from six days ago: Hammer-6 went in confident and only one came out, screaming about electricity, and had to be cut down from the fence.

**What Kade doesn't provide:**

- Any intention of entering the building. Not for any price.
- Information about his superior Captain Vorne's suspicious behaviour — not yet. If the Operatives return from the facility with evidence of the cover-up, he'll be more forthcoming.

**Perimeter survey:** The Operatives can walk the fence line before entry. Observations: makeshift civilian memorials at two fence sections. Graffiti. One perimeter camera visibly tracking movement — it should be offline. The loading bay roller door is partially raised. Dried blood inside, visible from the threshold.

---

### Scene 4 — Descent

The Operatives enter the facility. From this point, the environment itself is a threat.

**Ground Level / Loading Bay:**
Empty pallets. Abandoned SLA shipping crates. Dried blood trail leading inward and down. Hammer-6's last bootprints in the dust. The air smells of ozone.

**Sub-Level 1 — Operations Control Room:**
Banks of dead monitors. One terminal flickers. The security footage on the flickering screen shows the Operatives — in the positions they're currently standing — but the timestamp reads 47 minutes in the future. Something is watching and projecting. The terminal logs show the facility's final shutdown sequence was never completed.

**Sub-Level 1 — Worker Dormitories:**
Personal effects. Photographs. A child's toy on a bunk. One locker contains Trell's handwritten incident note, left during Hammer-6's entry: *"NOT ELECTRICAL. ALIVE. INSIDE THE WALLS. DON'T TRUST THE LIGHTS."*

**Sub-Level 1 — Stairwells:**
The deeper they go, the more the air hums. Residual energy. Ebb-sensitive Operatives (Ebon, Brainwasters) will feel something vast and wrong pulling at the edges of their perception. The boundary between solid reality and something else is thin here and getting thinner with depth.

---

### Scene 5 — The Deep Level

Sub-Level 3. The facility's true purpose becomes undeniable.

**Primary Generator Hall:**
A cathedral of dead industry. Massive silent turbines. Catwalks overhead in shadow. Reality feels wrong: voices of people who aren't there, glimpses of movement in reflective surfaces, the sensation of being observed by something enormously patient. The bodies of two Hammer-6 Operatives are here — desiccated, drained of moisture and electrical activity, faces frozen in terror.

This is where Tomás Keller patrols. He will ambush. He knows every shadow, every approach angle, every blind spot on every catwalk. He's fast, strong, and directed by the entity's will — but fragments of his original consciousness still fight for control. If the Operatives use electrical countermeasures or the entity's attention is divided, Keller may briefly hesitate, garble a warning, or attempt to harm himself to break the connection.

**Concealed Research Wing:**
Hidden behind a false maintenance panel on Sub-Level 2 (between levels 1 and 3). Access requires a keycard, found either on Vasquez's person or on the body of a researcher in the dormitory level. Inside: whiteboards covered in equations mixing thermodynamics with Ebb theory, dissection tables with restraints, organic matter in glowing suspension jars, and Dr. Vasquez's research journal — the full documented history of the experiment and the moment of catastrophic failure, in her own handwriting. Her personal terminal (password: "ailuJ") contains the embezzlement records and the complete containment collapse procedure.

**Dr. Vasquez** moves through the research wing and upper generator levels. She speaks in overlapping voices. She will attempt to lure Operatives deeper rather than attack directly — she is the entity's voice, its diplomat, its deception. Physical contact with her initiates energy draining. An Ebb-sensitive Operative who attempts mental contact may reach the fragment of Vasquez that remains trapped and suffering — she wants to die, cannot act against the Current's will, but can provide the containment override sequence.

---

### Scene 6 — The Rupture Chamber

Sub-Level 3, deepest accessible section. A spherical chamber, 20 metres across, walls scorched black. At its centre: a pulsing tear in reality approximately 2 metres wide, crackling with dark energy. This is the entity's anchor point.

Near the Rupture: gravity fluctuates. Time feels wrong — sluggish, inconsistent. The boundary between alive and dead blurs. Ebb users should be warned to proceed carefully.

The Hollow Current will be fully present here. It rarely manifests physically — when it does, witnesses describe it as a shape made of lightning and void, with too many things that aren't quite eyes. Primarily it acts through electrical manipulation, thralls, and environmental distortion.

**Resolving the Rupture** (see Resolution Options) requires either using the research notes to overload the containment field generators — a technically demanding task under extreme pressure — or making other choices with their own costs.

## GM Notes

**Tone:** Corporate horror. The monster is real but it isn't the worst thing here — SLA Industries created the conditions for this disaster and will bury the squad along with the evidence if it suits them. Every NPC except Trell and Kade has an agenda that doesn't put the Operatives first. Reward paranoia.

**Pacing:** The investigation before entry should feel like the last moment of safety. Once inside, time pressure builds naturally — the entity is watching from the moment they cross the threshold. The flickering monitor showing future-timestamped footage is the moment the players should feel genuinely unsettled. Don't rush past it.

**The Entity:** Never describe The Hollow Current clearly. Glimpses only. It works through others. When it does appear physically, it should be profoundly wrong — not frightening in a conventional way, but deeply dissonant, like something the mind refuses to process correctly.

**Vasquez's Trapped Fragment:** This is the emotional core of the scenario. If an Ebb operative makes contact, play Vasquez's trapped consciousness as exhausted and lucid — she's been a witness to everything the entity has done through her body. She knows she's beyond saving. She can give the overload sequence and a final piece of information: the name and false identity of the fourth researcher who escaped. She asks only one thing in return.

**Keller's Hesitation:** Use this sparingly. One moment of Keller briefly fighting the entity's control, reaching out with something almost like his original self, is worth more than three. If the Operatives notice and respond with compassion rather than just firepower, they'll remember it.

**IA Agent Slade:** He should appear as a voice first — a contact call offering "clarification" on the BPN. He knows they've been inside. He's offering bonuses for discrete handling before they're too deep to refuse. He is polite, professional, and utterly chilling. His termination authorisation is a clock — once triggered, the Operatives have a second hostile to manage alongside everything in the facility.

**The Rain:** It never stops on Mort. Every exterior scene should note it. Inside the facility, the distant sound of rain on the roof is a reminder that the normal world still exists — and is getting further away with every level they descend.

## Adaptations & Variants

**Shorter version (3 hours):** Remove the Trell interview and Kade scenes as required pre-entry beats — give their information via BPN briefing documents instead. Begin with the Operatives already at the perimeter. Cuts depth but keeps all the core scenario beats.

**Escalation (sequel hook):** The fourth researcher — the one who escaped — is living under a false identity somewhere on Mort with everything she saw and everything she copied from the research files before the Rupture. If the Operatives chose the Exposure route, she surfaces to corroborate. If they chose Demolition or Containment, she is the loose end IA will eventually come for. Either way, she's a future scenario.

**Cross-system:** With adaptation, this scenario works in any corporate horror setting. The Hollow Current is a sufficiently generic trans-dimensional entity that the Ebb-specific elements can be replaced with equivalent supernatural frameworks without structural change.
