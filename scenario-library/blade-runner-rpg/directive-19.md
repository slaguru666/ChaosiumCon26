# DIRECTIVE 19
*A Blade Runner Roleplaying Game scenario for six Blade Runners*

---

## Overview

- **System:** The Blade Runner Roleplaying Game (Free League Publishing)
- **Setting:** Los Angeles, **2037** — ten years after the Blackout, twelve before *Blade Runner 2049*
- **Tone:** Cold noir. Investigation in Act One, action in Act Two, a tower showdown and a quiet escape in Act Three.
- **Maximum Runtime:** 3 hours 30 minutes
- **Players:** 6 (pre-generated Blade Runners — mix of human and Nexus-9)
- **Art Style:** Moebius / Syd Mead — ink linework with colour wash, heavy chiaroscuro, neon accent lighting, rain on glass

---

## Plot Summary *(GM Only — the full truth)*

In **2027**, between the chaos of the Blackout (2022) and the rise of the Wallace Corporation monopoly (2028), a covert operation called **Directive 19** was run out of an unmarked office in the City Hall sub-basement. Its handlers were a four-person cell: a senior city aide, a Tyrell Corp restructuring lawyer, a senior LAPD officer, and an agent of what would soon be Wallace's lobbying apparatus.

Their problem was simple. Replicant Prohibition had been law since 2023. Wallace had quietly bought the Tyrell salvage rights and was lobbying for the right to manufacture a new generation under licence. But several political and corporate figures stood in his way — and could not be removed legally.

The solution: three combat-model Nexus-8 replicants who had survived the Blackout and gone underground in the salt flats north of the basin. Captured, blackmailed (their faces were on a kill list, their fingerprints in old Tyrell files), and conscripted under coercion, they were given a list of targets and turned loose.

Over eighteen months, the three replicants killed **seven people**:
1. **Senator Marcus Yim** — sponsor of the Permanent Replicant Prohibition Act. Killed in a "car accident" on the Sepulveda Pass.
2. **Aurelius Tyrell** — Eldon Tyrell's grand-nephew, sole remaining family member, refused to sign over salvage rights. Killed in a "home invasion" in Pasadena.
3. **Marisol Vance** — Mayor's Chief of Staff, was building a corruption case against the City Hall cell. Killed in a "mugging" in Westlake. *(NB: surname is also Dmitri Vance's. Coincidence at the table — let players notice or not.)*
4. **Hashem Lin** — a Tyrell-contracted bio-engineer who had been quietly archiving evidence of the cell's activity. Vanished. Body never recovered. *(Saoirse Lin's father.)*
5. **Detective Aaron Ostrow** *(later Judge Ostrow)* — LAPD homicide, was four files away from the truth. Bought, not killed, in 2027. Killed in 2037 by Wren when she found out he had been bought. *(Talia Ostrow's uncle.)*
6. **Helen Park** — independent journalist, writing a piece on the Yim accident. Killed in a "drug overdose" in Echo Park. *(Lin Ai was her co-author. Lin Ai survived because she missed the meeting.)*
7. **Esfir Volkov** — union organiser at a Tyrell-adjacent automation plant. Killed in a "fire." *(Distantly related to Dr Volkov in the present — his cousin.)*

By the time Wallace's monopoly was rubber-stamped in 2028, the cell's work was done. The three replicants were taken to a warehouse near the **Saltgrove Industrial Estate** on the north salt flats, ostensibly for new mission orders. Two were executed there — shot in the back of the head, bodies dissolved in industrial solvent. **The third — Wren — escaped, killed one of the cell's lieutenants, and disappeared into the high desert.**

She spent ten years preparing.

**Three weeks before this scenario opens**, she returned to the basin and began executing the surviving handlers and their lieutenants. By the time the scenario begins, she has killed two: Captain Lazlo Voss (Cortez's chief of security in 2027) and Judge Aaron Ostrow (the bought detective). Three more are on her list, including the architect — **Deputy Mayor Helena Cortez**, the former aide, now a mayoral candidate.

A second party is also moving. **Wallace Internal Security** has belatedly realised the operation is unravelling and has dispatched a Nexus-9 "cleaner" named **Halden** to retire Wren and any Blade Runners stupid enough to keep the file open.

And Wren is not alone. She has spent two years training a human apprentice: **Sera Park**, daughter of Helen Park, the journalist murdered in 2027. Sera was nine when her mother died. She is now twenty-six and a competent killer.

The PCs are LAPD Blade Runners assigned to retire Wren. They will discover, slowly, that the people who employ them are the people on her list.

**By the end of the scenario, Wren escapes** — this is a design decision baked into Act Three. The question is what the PCs decide to do with what they have learned, and at what cost.

## Player Experience *(What the table lives)*

A high-profile murder — a retired LAPD captain, drowned in his own bathtub on the 41st floor of the Bradbury Tower. The killer is clearly a replicant. The mark on the mirror reads **DIR-019**. Lt. Greer wants the case wrapped in 72 hours, clean.

In Act One the PCs work the scene cold. They pull bank records that lead to a shell company tied to a deputy mayor. A second body is found across town — a judge, the same mark. A journalist no one believed has had this story for ten years. A back-alley cutter in Chinatown patched up "the tall woman" six months ago.

In Act Two the violence begins. The cutter is dead when they arrive. A second Blade Runner — federal, polite, lying — tells them to back off. They see Wren for the first time. She doesn't engage. A young human woman ambushes them in a parking structure. A salt-flats warehouse holds the records of a ten-year-old execution. Someone close to one of the PCs dies.

In Act Three they reach the fundraiser tower. Wren is going for Cortez. Sera is on the roof completing a broadcast. Halden is hunting all of them. The team will split. They will choose what they save and what they let burn. On a wet terrace twenty stories above the lights, one of them will be alone with Wren for ninety seconds — and have a decision to make that no rule book covers.

By the closing image — sirens below, a body bag being zipped, Greer arriving with a face like a closed door — they will know that whichever way they jumped, the city does not care.

## World Background — Los Angeles, 2037

### What It Looks Like

Constant rain. The basin's air-recyclers have been failing in micro-cycles since 2031 — every few days the precipitation runs slightly acid and people who can afford to stay inside do. Most can't. The smog ceiling sits at about 600 feet and the upper floors of any tower above that are above the cloud line; Wallace's executive class lives in a different sky from the rest of the city.

Neon at street level — Korean, Mandarin, Spanish, English in roughly that frequency on signage. Holographic billboards layered on the older neon, sometimes glitching together. The streets are wet and crowded. People wear filter masks more often than they did three years ago. The traffic is mostly ground-bound — spinners exist but require a class-7 licence almost no one outside the LAPD, Wallace, or the wealthy can get.

### The Blackout and What Came After

In **2022**, an electromagnetic event of disputed origin wiped most of the city's digital records — births, deaths, criminal histories, financial records, replicant manufacture data. The official line is that it was a Replicant Liberation Front attack. Privately, most people who think about it suspect a Tyrell Corp employee panicking about pending lawsuits.

After the Blackout came the **Replicant Prohibition Act of 2023**, banning manufacture for five years. Surviving Nexus-8s — replicants with open lifespans — went underground or were retired. The Wallace Corporation purchased Tyrell salvage rights in 2025 and lobbied for prohibition's repeal. **In 2028 the Prohibition was lifted under strict licensing.** Wallace has been the only licence-holder ever since.

The years between — 2025 to 2028 — are the era of Directive 19. The Blackout-wiped records were a convenient covering, and the Prohibition-era illegality of any replicant operation meant the cell could conscript Nexus-8s and dispose of them with no possible legal trail.

### Blade Runners in 2037

The LAPD's **Rep-Detect** division is small — twelve detectives, two captains, one lieutenant. Most Blade Runners are humans with a specialty in identifying and retiring replicants. **Since 2034, Wallace Corp has supplied four Nexus-9 replicants to Rep-Detect on permanent secondment**, ostensibly to assist with cases requiring physical capacity beyond a human officer's. Wallace says they are loyal by design. Many human Blade Runners suspect this is exactly the problem.

A Blade Runner who retires a replicant earns a **Promotion Point** — a credit that can be spent for bonuses on later rolls, but which carries an unspoken accumulation cost: a slow erosion of empathy, registered on the character sheet as **Humanity loss**. For replicant Blade Runners, retiring another replicant carries an additional weight the rules don't quite name.

### Wallace Corporation

The only legal replicant manufacturer. Headquartered downtown in the spike-tower that replaced the Tyrell pyramid. Niander Wallace himself is rarely seen. The Corporation's lobbying apparatus, supply chain, and security division operate as a near-state within the state. Halden — the cleaner sent in Act Two — comes from this apparatus.

### Cortez and the Mayoral Race

**Deputy Mayor Helena Cortez** is the front-runner for the 2037 mayoral primary. Her platform is "Order and Renewal" — she ran the redevelopment of the Sub-District Three corridor, brought light-rail back, has Wallace's quiet backing and the Police Federation's endorsement. The primary is in nine days. Her campaign fundraiser, on the 88th floor of the Sandoval Tower, is in three.

She was a thirty-year-old policy aide in 2027. She believed Directive 19 was a temporary measure to prevent worse violence and a worse future. She believes this still. She is not a cartoon — she is the most dangerous person in the scenario because her conviction is genuine.

## Running Los Angeles 2037 — GM Texture Guide

### What People Wear
- Long coats. Synthetic leather, oilcloth, or layered woven plastic that beads water. Hoods.
- Filter scarves — half-mask cloth across the mouth, drawn down for conversation, drawn up on the street.
- Subdermal augments are common at the lower levels — visible ridges along jaw, neck, temple. The wealthy have them removed or hidden under more expensive cosmetic surgery.
- Police wear navy with reflective amber piping. Detectives wear what they want; Blade Runners are detectives.

### How People Talk
- "Skin-job" is racial — replicants say it about themselves bitterly; humans use it casually and the LAPD policy is to discourage but not punish.
- "Off-world" is the upward direction. "Down here" is everyone else.
- "Pre-Blackout" is a marker of authenticity ("pre-Blackout glass," "pre-Blackout coffee"). Things genuinely from before 2022 carry a premium.
- "Wallace" is a verb at the lower levels — "they got Wallaced" means a company was bought or eliminated.
- Cigarettes are still legal, expensive, and smoked. Coffee is a luxury good. Real coffee is a status marker.

### What Things Cost
- A noodle bowl: 12 credits
- A real coffee: 80 credits
- A black-market replicant patch-up: 4,000 credits cash, no questions
- A precinct comm with priority access: issued
- A spinner ride within district: 250 credits
- A Nexus-9 secondment to Wallace's books: too much

### Sensory Language by Location
- **Bradbury Tower (Voss penthouse):** rain on floor-to-ceiling glass, copper-stained marble, the faint ozone of a malfunctioning humidity scrubber, a wet footprint on heated stone, no smell of blood — Voss was drowned, not cut.
- **Persian Quarter (Lin Ai's noodle bar):** wet wool, steamed broth, fluorescent buzz, the slap of a wet card pulled from a freezer, the smell of pickled garlic and warm rice vinegar.
- **Chinatown (Volkov's clinic):** antiseptic and engine oil, a constant low hum from the autoclave, a cracked surveillance camera in a corner that hasn't worked in three years, the smell of melted plastic from a soldering iron just turned off.
- **Saltgrove Industrial Estate (the warehouse):** dry salt cracking under boots, wind through corrugated steel, no rain — they are above the basin smog — the smell of nothing and the absence of any human sound for two miles.
- **Sandoval Tower (fundraiser):** champagne and ozone, the hush of expensive carpet absorbing footsteps, the polite quiet of a room where everyone is watching everyone, the smell of orchid and tonic water.
- **The terrace (Act Three climax):** wet stone, the smell of distant ozone from the spinner traffic above, the city below as a single low orange smear under cloud.

### What Technology Looks Like
- CRT monitors are common at lower-budget locations — they survived the Blackout when flat-panels didn't.
- Phones are physical — a slab of black glass, mostly voice-driven, rarely visual. Calls drop. Encryption is expensive.
- Cars are real cars. Spinners are above. The traffic noise on the ground level is petrol engines, ozone from electric models, and the deep thrum of cargo haulers.
- Police-issue Voigt-Kampff units are wheeled-frame, briefcase-sized, take six minutes to run a full test. Replicants in 2037 have learned to pass partial tests; a senior Blade Runner trusts a real conversation over a machine.

### Opening — Read Aloud or Paraphrase

> *"It's been raining for eleven days. The basin doesn't drain like it used to and the storm drains around the Bradbury Tower are running orange with rust. It's 3:14 in the morning. Your comms went off forty minutes ago. By the time you got here, the bodies were cold."*
>
> *"Bradbury Tower. Floor 41. The penthouse belongs — belonged — to Captain Lazlo Voss, retired LAPD, currently chief of private security for the Cortez mayoral campaign. There are three dead in the corridor. There is one in the bathtub. There is something on the mirror."*

### Player Briefing

The PCs are Blade Runners from the LAPD Rep-Detect division. The night is rain. They arrived together — the call routed through their lieutenant — and they are standing on a heated marble floor in a dead man's penthouse, looking at a mark on a mirror that reads **DIR-019**.

They do not yet know what Directive 19 is. **Nobody alive knows the full truth — not even Lt. Greer.** The PCs will be the first to assemble it.

### Character Introductions

Spend ten minutes letting players introduce themselves in character. Encourage them to address the scene — what they see, what they smell, what they note. The Forensic Specialist (Saoirse) should have first crack at the body in the bath. The Combat Replicant (Cole) should sweep the corridor and the two dead bodyguards. Iris should be quietly cataloguing — she has done this before.

### Atmosphere Notes

- **Music:** Vangelis "Tears in the Rain" loop, or any solo piano on a five-minute repeat.
- **Lighting:** Lower the table light if you can. The penthouse is mostly lit by the city outside the rain.
- **Pace:** Slow. Encourage long looks. Discourage immediate dice rolls — the first act is about looking, not testing.

## ACT ONE — The Case *(~60 minutes)*
> **Goal:** Pure investigation. Establish the crime, the victim, the missing pieces. No combat. The PCs leave Act One with a list of names, a shell company, a suspect they cannot quite see, and the growing certainty that someone in their own building is on the wrong side.

### Summary

The PCs work the Voss penthouse crime scene. They learn about a second body — Judge Aaron Ostrow — killed across town the same night, same mark. They follow forensic and financial leads. A journalist no one has believed for a decade hands them a list of nine names. A back-alley cutter is mentioned. Lt. Greer applies pressure to close fast.

The act ends with a phone call from Volkov, the cutter, asking to meet — and the line cutting mid-sentence.

### Location: Voss Penthouse, Bradbury Tower, Floor 41

- **Description:** Restored pre-Blackout architecture, floor-to-ceiling glass on three walls, heated marble, copper fixtures. Voss had taste he didn't earn.
- **Atmosphere:** Rain on the glass. The temperature is too warm — the humidity scrubber failed at 11pm, raising the indoor humidity by 12%. Everything feels slightly damp.
- **Key Details / Secrets:**
  - Two bodyguards dead in the entry corridor, necks broken, professional. Both are ex-LAPD — **served in 2027** under Voss.
  - Voss in the bath, fully dressed in a navy suit, drowned. No defensive wounds. He let his killer into the bathroom.
  - On the mirror, in shaving cream: **DIR-019**.
  - One muddy boot-print on the kitchen tile — military tread, women's size 9. Wren's.
  - A wet footprint on the marble in the bedroom. Different print. Smaller. **Sera's** — though the PCs do not yet know there are two killers.
  - Voss's tablet is on the bedside table. Unlocked. Open to a banking app. *(See Clue 2.)*
  - Voss had three deadbolts and a panic room. He let the killer in willingly. *(Insight: he knew her, or she had access he trusted.)*

### Location: City Morgue, Sub-Basement Two

- **Description:** Concrete, fluorescent, smells of bleach and decay-suppressant. The morgue clerk on the night shift is named **Marisol Yu** — friendly, talks too much.
- **Atmosphere:** Cold. The Voss body is on slab 3. Aaron Ostrow's body is on slab 7. He has not been claimed yet.
- **Key Details / Secrets:**
  - Both bodies bear the **DIR-019** mark — Voss in shaving cream, Ostrow tattooed onto his chest with a med-tool from his own kit while he was paralysed but conscious. **He saw who killed him.** Coroner says paralytic was a Wallace-grade synaptic blocker — military, not street.
  - Talia Ostrow's badge identifies her as Aaron's niece. Marisol Yu, awkwardly: *"You're family — you should be the one to look through his effects."* (Talia inherits the files — see Clue 5.)
  - Bodyguards' files: both ex-LAPD, both honourably discharged in 2028, both onto Voss's private security payroll within three weeks of their discharge.

### Location: The Bowl, Persian Quarter — Lin Ai's Noodle Bar

- **Description:** A three-table noodle bar under a pedestrian overpass on Fairfax. Pakistani Persian fusion, the owner doesn't ask. Lin Ai eats here every night at 1am, alone, by choice.
- **Atmosphere:** Steam. Fluorescent buzz. The smell of pickled garlic and warm rice vinegar.
- **Key Details / Secrets:**
  - **Lin Ai** is a 49-year-old independent journalist. Once mainstream; now writes for an underground newsletter with a print run of 800.
  - She has been writing about "the 2027 disappearances" for a decade. She is considered a crank.
  - She hands the PCs **Handout BR19-H01-A** — the list of nine. *(See Clue 3.)*
  - She does **not** know what DIR-019 stands for. But she has been seeing it scrawled in Voss's neighbourhood graffiti for three weeks. She thought it was a teen tag.
  - She knew Voss's professional life. She knew Ostrow professionally — interviewed him in 2028 about an unsolved case. She did not get a clean answer.
  - She is afraid. She has been afraid for ten years. She still meets the PCs because Helen Park was her co-author and her friend.

### NPCs

#### Lieutenant Marcus Greer — LAPD Rep-Detect, Pressure-from-Above
- **Role:** PCs' commanding officer. The first obstacle they don't quite see.

## ACT TWO — The Trail *(~90 minutes)*
> **Goal:** The action act. The PCs see the antagonists for the first time. Wren walks past them. Sera ambushes them. Halden introduces himself. A contact dies. Cortez and the Directive 19 truth come into focus. By the end, the team knows the fundraiser tonight is where it ends.

### Summary

Volkov is dead when they arrive — killed by **Halden**, who is waiting. Halden gives them a polite warning. They follow leads to **Elias Pham**, the last living name on Lin Ai's list. **Wren is there before them.** She kills Pham in front of them and leaves them alive — *"You're not on the list. Don't make me put you there."*

In a subsequent scene, **Sera** ambushes one PC subgroup — a parking structure or rooftop firefight. If captured or wounded into talking, she gives them everything: the names, the warehouse, Cortez. If she escapes, they find the warehouse through Aaron Ostrow's files (Clue 5 from Act One) and Lin Ai's research.

The **Saltgrove Industrial Estate warehouse** holds the truth: the execution site. Wren has been there recently. She has left documents for the PCs to find — including handout BR19-H02-B, a three-line note.

Late in Act Two, the **personal jeopardy beat fires** — a contact close to one of the PCs is killed. The GM chooses which PC and which contact, picked for maximum impact, before the session.

The team converges on the conclusion: the fundraiser tonight is the night.

### Location: Dr Volkov's Clinic, Chinatown

- **Description:** A converted shop unit beneath a noodle place. Heavy steel door, panel of black-market medical kit visible through the gap.
- **Atmosphere:** Antiseptic and engine oil. The autoclave is still humming. A scalpel is on the floor, point first. The blood is still wet.
- **Key Details / Secrets:**
  - Volkov is in the back room. Throat opened. **Not Wren's signature** — Wren's kills are quiet, clinical, and signed. This is fast and brutal. Different killer.
  - **Halden** is in the front room when the PCs arrive. He is alone. He is wearing a charcoal suit and standing at the desk turning a pen between his fingers. He looks up when the PCs enter and smiles.
- **Artwork Asset:** BR19-ART-02

### Halden — The Cleaner *(see full NPC profile below)*

When the PCs enter the clinic, Halden produces a credential — federal, looks real, isn't — and tells them their case is now "subject to interagency oversight" and they should stand down. He is unfailingly polite. He tells them, by name, where each of them lives. He thanks them for their service. He walks out into the rain.

This is the moment the PCs realise there is a second predator on the board.

### Location: Elias Pham's Safehouse, San Pedro Marina

- **Description:** A retrofitted boat house on a derelict marina, one of three identical units along the wharf. Pham — last living name on Lin Ai's list — has been hiding here for three years, fully off-grid.
- **Atmosphere:** Wet wood, rust, the slap of small boats against pylons. The lights inside are not on. They should be.
- **Key Details / Secrets:**
  - Pham is at his kitchen table when the PCs enter. He is already dead — Wren is standing behind him, holding the chair. **She has waited for them.**
  - She speaks. Three lines maximum: *"You're not on the list. Don't make me put you there. He paid for the others."*
  - She goes out the window. The window is on the third floor, over water. She is gone before the first PC reaches it.
  - On the kitchen table: Pham's confession — written in his own hand under duress, naming Cortez, Voss, Tyrell-lawyer Hadley Brent (already dead, 2031), and a fourth name redacted by water damage. **Handout BR19-H02-B** is here, left for the PCs by Wren.

### Location: Saltgrove Industrial Estate, North Salt Flats

- **Description:** Two hours north of central LA. Above the smog ceiling — the sky is genuinely visible here, hard and white. A row of corrugated steel warehouses, mostly abandoned. Warehouse 7 is the one.
- **Atmosphere:** No rain. No traffic noise. The wind through the corrugated steel is loud. Salt cracks under boots.
- **Key Details / Secrets:**
  - The interior of Warehouse 7 has been swept clean, but **Wren has prepared it as a memorial** — three salt-encrusted photographs nailed to a beam, two of her sisters (dead), one of herself. Beneath each, a name written in her own hand: **Kira, Nine, herself**.
  - On the floor under a tarp: the **ledger page** (Handout BR19-H02-A) — payments from Pelican Holdings to a "Saltgrove Logistics LLC" in 2027-2028, with a list of dates that correlate with the seven Directive 19 killings.

- **Artwork Asset:** BR19-ART-03

## ACT THREE — The Tower *(~30 minutes)*
> **Goal:** The showdown. Three converging tracks. The team splits. Wren escapes. The decisions made tonight will be carried forward.

### Summary

The fundraiser is in full swing on the 88th floor of the Sandoval Tower. Cortez is on schedule to speak in twelve minutes. **Wren is descending toward her** through the service corridors. **Sera is on the roof**, two floors above, setting up the broadcast relay. **Halden is in the building**, hunting all three.

The team will split. Three tracks, three subgroups. Whoever ends up on the 88th-floor terrace alone with Wren in the closing beats gets the terrace scene.

The act resolves at minute 28 with the broadcast either landing or being stopped, Cortez either reaching her speech or not, and Wren disappearing into the rain.

### Location: Sandoval Tower, 88th Floor — Fundraiser

- **Description:** A converted observation level — domed glass, expensive carpet, white-clothed tables, three hundred guests in formal black. Above the smog line.
- **Atmosphere:** Champagne. Quiet music. The hum of a thousand polite conversations. Outside the glass, the city is a low orange smear below.
- **Key Details / Secrets:**
  - Cortez is in the green room at the rear, with two staff, scheduled to take the podium at 21:30.
  - The service corridors behind the green room are not on the guest map.
  - The terrace runs around the south face. The doors to it are unlocked. The wind is hard.

### Location: Sandoval Tower, Roof

- **Description:** A flat industrial roof above the dome, with an HVAC bank and a service ladder up from the 90th-floor maintenance level. Sera has hacked into the building's emergency broadcast relay.
- **Atmosphere:** Hard wind. Rain horizontal at this height. No one comes up here.
- **Key Details / Secrets:**
  - Sera is set up at the relay terminal with a laptop and a hardline jack. The broadcast file is loaded. It runs 4 minutes 11 seconds.
  - If she runs the broadcast, every comm in the basin receives it as an emergency override. **The Directive 19 truth goes public.**

### Location: The Terrace — 88th-Floor South Face

- **Description:** A six-foot stone shelf running the length of the south face. Glass on one side, rain on the other, twenty stories of nothing below.
- **Atmosphere:** Wet stone. The wind dies for a moment when the figures step out — the dome breaks the airflow.
- **Key Details / Secrets:**
  - This is where the climax conversation happens. Wren ends up here — wounded, calm, with a PC.

### NPCs / Antagonists

#### Helena Cortez — Deputy Mayor, Architect of Directive 19
- **Role:** The political enemy. The person Wren is going to kill, or not.
- **Physical Description:** Mid-forties, black hair shoulder-length and immaculate, a dark green dress chosen to read maternal and serious in equal measure. A faint trembling in her left hand that she has hidden for ten years.
- **Motivation:** To win the primary. To bury Directive 19. To live with what she did by continuing to believe she was right.
- **What She Knows:** Everything. She has run this operation. She is its author.
- **Disposition:** Cordial, exhausted, hard to hate face-to-face. She will offer the PCs a future if they help her tonight. She will mean it. She does not see herself as a villain.
- **Mechanics:**
  - STR C | AGI C | WIT A | EMP B
  - Manipulation A, Insight A, Connect A
  - Unarmed. Her security detail is downstairs. Up here, she is alone with a clutch and a microphone.

### The Terrace Scene *(The Heart of the Climax)*

When one PC is alone with Wren on the terrace — wounded by the night, by the case, or by their own choice — Wren speaks. The scene is a conversation, not a fight. Run it slowly.

**Wren says, in sequence:**

> *"You came after me. That's your job. I'm not angry."*
>
> *"I had sisters. Their names were Kira and Nine. We were ordered to kill seven people, and we did, and then they took us to a warehouse and put my sisters' heads on the concrete and shot them. I ran. I have been running for ten years."*
>
> *"I know what you came here for. I know what you'll be told to write in the report. I know the man inside [pointing through the glass — Cortez] would have killed my sisters herself if the cell hadn't done it for her. He believes he was right. He still believes it. That's the thing that takes the air out of me."*
>
> *[Then, looking directly at the PC]:*
> *"Do you know what your own model number is? Do you know whose face is in your wedding photograph? Do you know whose voice you hear when you don't sleep? You don't. None of us do."*
>
> *"I'm going now. You can stop me, or you can let me go. Whichever you choose, the next thing you do tomorrow morning is the only thing about you that matters."*

**Then, regardless of what the PC chooses:**
- If the PC tries to retire her, run **one opposed roll** — Firearms vs Wren's Mobility. If the PC wins, Wren falls from the terrace, body not recovered. If the PC loses, Wren disarms them and goes over the edge into the rain. Either way: she is gone.
- If the PC lets her go, she nods once, says nothing more, and steps off the terrace edge backwards into the rain. No one sees her again that night.

### Story Hooks

- If the players freeze at the climax, **Sera fires the broadcast** off her own initiative. The truth lands. The city sees Directive 19.
- If they push for an arrest of Cortez, **Halden kills her first**. Wallace cleaning house.
- If they let it all go, **Greer arrives in the lobby** as they come down — he understands. He nods. He files the case as "open, unresolved." A promotion is on the table. The PCs will live with it.

## GM Notes — Running Directive 19

### The Tone Problem (And How To Avoid It)

This scenario can be over-explained into flatness. The plot is dense, the politics are real, and there is a temptation to keep clarifying things for the players. Don't. The best version of this scenario at the table has long silences, slow conversations, and players reaching conclusions a beat before you would have stated them. **Let them put the pieces together. Let them mishear and re-hear. Let Wren be opaque until the terrace.**

If you find yourself summarising what the cell did, stop. Hand them the ledger. Read the names. Let the names do the work.

### Six Players — Splitting the Party

Six is at the upper edge of comfortable for this game. Plan to split:
- Act One: bodyguards / body / financial trail can each take a subgroup
- Act Two: marina / salt flats / Sera ambush can run as parallel scenes — cut between them every 5 minutes
- Act Three: the three converging tracks at the tower are designed to split — let it happen, don't fight it

### The Terrace Scene — Rehearse It

This is the highest-leverage minute of the session. Pick the PC who ends up there carefully — usually the one with the strongest personal hook to what Wren says. Read the lines slowly. Do not let the player roll dice unless they push for the retire. **Let the conversation be the climax, not the dice.**

If multiple PCs are present on the terrace, focus the lines on one. The others can react.

### The Personal Jeopardy Beat — Use Once

Pick the PC, pick the contact, pick the moment before the session. Do not improvise this on the night — improvised emotional beats land flatter and risk feeling cheap. The death should be off-screen, discovered. Not depicted.

Default candidates:
- Iris's old partner's widow, who Iris has kept loose contact with — found by Halden, dead in her apartment.
- Saoirse's mother — already widowed by Hashem's 2027 disappearance — found by Halden, dead in hers.
- Talia's other uncle — Aaron's brother, still alive at the start — found dead, scene staged to look like Aaron's.
- Dmitri's old commanding officer — the one who supported him after the 2027 blacklisting — found in a parking garage.
- Marc and Cole — replicants — both have a Wallace handler who has been "supportive" of their transition; either could be removed, leaving them more alone in a way that lands differently than for a human PC.

### Greer — A Real Person, Not a Cartoon

Greer is the easiest character to play as a sneering bureaucrat and that flattens him. He took the envelope twelve years ago, didn't know what was happening then, learned what had happened later, has lived with it. He loves his daughter. He visits his wife in the hospital on Sundays. He has been waiting for a knock on the door for a decade. **The knock comes tonight.**

Play him tired. Play him afraid. Let his sabotage be small at first — a misfiled report, a delayed comm. Let it escalate as he panics. Do not have him point a gun at the PCs — he wouldn't. He sends Hara, or worse, Halden.

### Halden — The Restraint Test

The temptation is to use Halden every time the action slows down. Don't. He should appear three times: at Volkov's clinic, perhaps once in the middle of Act Two as a phone call or a parked spinner on a roof opposite the team, and at the tower. If the PCs engage him directly without overwhelming advantage, they will lose. The point of Halden is not the fight — it's the awareness that he is in the room.

If you are running the scenario as a one-shot, kill him in Act Three. If you are running it as the start of a campaign, let him walk.

### Cortez — Don't Make Her a Cartoon

Cortez is the easiest character to flatten. Resist. She has to believe she was right. Her redemption arc never came. She thinks she saved the city from a worse 2028 and she will say so calmly to anyone who asks. The horror is not that she did it — it's that, in her position, she would do it again.

A specific detail before the session: give her one small, private grace. A scholarship she funds anonymously. A pet she rescues. A music she loves. Whatever lands for your table. The horror is that real people who do real harm are also full people.

### What Happens If The Team Tries to Arrest Cortez Early

Halden intervenes. A bystander dies. One PC is wounded. The case is taken from Rep-Detect by federal credentials that are not federal and not credentials. The PCs are sent home for the night. **They have until 21:00 tonight to fix this off the books.**

### What Happens If The Team Decides to Help Wren

It's a valid ending. Run it. Be honest about the cost — a Blade Runner who helps a hunted replicant cannot stay on the force. The Promotion Points lost are real. The choice is real. Don't punish the players for making it; let the cost speak for itself in the epilogue.

### What Happens If A PC Tries To Retire Wren On The Terrace

Run the one opposed roll. **She wins, or she falls and isn't recovered.** Either way, she is gone. The dice do not decide whether she lives — they decide what she costs. If the PC wins the roll, they have killed her in their own mind and will not sleep. If the PC loses the roll, she walks away and they file the report knowing she beat them.

This is the part of the scenario that most needs the GM to be honest with the players before the session. The escape is structural. The choice is what matters.

### Closing the Session

End on the closing image, not on a debrief. Read the closing scene. Let the table sit with it for thirty seconds. Then ask each player to say one sentence about what their character does tomorrow morning. Don't comment. Don't explain. Close the book.
