## Plot Summary *(GM Only)*

Veltris Solutions, a mid-tier data and logistics corporation, purchased the derelict Sutter-Vane Water Reclamation Plant in Sub-District 9 eighteen months ago as a shell transaction through three subsidiaries. When their survey team entered Sub-Level 9, they found it already occupied — by something that had been there far longer than the city above it.

The entity beneath the plant is a Spawn of the Lurker at the Threshold: a semi-corporeal Mythos predator that predates Neo-Ashford by centuries, possibly millennia. It does not think. It does not want in any human sense. It extends, consumes, and grows, using organic filament networks to alter its environment and condition nearby organisms toward behaviour that serves its spread. Forty-seven people have vanished from the surrounding six blocks in eighteen months. The local authorities have been paid to file these as gang violence statistics.

Veltris, rather than destroying what they found, began studying it. Then feeding it. Then integrating it into a low-level research programme under the directorship of Dr Yava Chen, who has since spent fourteen consecutive weeks on-site and is no longer entirely herself.

Damos Reth, a mid-level data analyst, accidentally accessed the unredacted research files six days ago. He copied what he could to a data-chip, deleted his access logs, and ran to the Undertow — the only place in Neo-Ashford where corporate surveillance has gaps. He has been hiding in the tunnels for four days. He does not know that the entity already knew he was there before he arrived.

Sable Morn, Veltris's handler, has hired the player team as deniable assets to retrieve Reth and the chip. She expects the operatives to be silenced afterward. She has a standing arrangement with Veltris black ops for exactly this scenario.

By the end of the night, the players will know too much — just like Reth did.

## Player Experience *(What the table lives)*

The operatives are sold a routine retrieval job: find a frightened data analyst, bring him back, or bring back what he was carrying. The Undertow is uncomfortable but familiar territory. The money is good.

The world cracks open slowly. First there is an odd smell. Then a child's warning they cannot explain. Then Reth's room — the handwritten notes, the photograph that will not resolve into a recognisable shape. Then the tunnels, where the walls are warm and alive, and something moved in the drone feed that is no longer there when they look.

By the time they find Reth, half-mad and telling them the entity already knows their names, the scenario has shifted from a corporate job to a survival question. And then it gives them a choice with no clean answer.

## World Background — Neo-Ashford, 2087

### What It Looks Like
Neo-Ashford is a city of permanent twilight. Twenty arcology towers — the tallest over eight hundred metres — rise above a persistent cloud layer of industrial smog and particulate haze. Above the cloud line: climate-controlled tiers with piped sunlight, filtered water, and manicured green space for the corporate elite. Below: the rest of the city, eleven million people, operating under artificial light, acid-tinged rain, and neon.

The architecture at street level is layered: the original twentieth-century city underneath, concrete brutalism from the 2030s over that, and the corporate construction of the 2060s and 70s bolted and cantilevered across the top. The result is a vertical geography where sub-levels, maintenance tunnels, and old drainage infrastructure form an underworld — the Undertow — that the city's official maps no longer fully acknowledge.

Rain falls most nights. Slightly caustic — not enough to burn skin, but enough to pit uncoated surfaces and leave a faint chemical tang. Most Undertow residents wear filter scarves out of habit.

### The Arcology System
The twenty arcology megablocks are owned by the eight dominant corporations. Living in an arcology is a condition of senior employment — corporations provide housing as leverage. Leaving corporate employment means leaving the arcology within sixty days. The remaining population lives in mid-rise and street-level zones where services are inconsistent and corporate security patrols only the commercial districts. Sub-districts are numbered by proximity to arcology access; the higher the number, the further from corporate power.

### Technology Distribution
Advanced but radically uneven. Corporations have gene-splice clinics, orbital launch capability, and predictive AI. A street-level resident of Sub-District 7 has a black-market augment from a chop shop, a wrist-embedded transit chip, and a second-hand device for public-net access on a good signal day.

**Black market augments** common in the Undertow: reflex-enhancing neural taps (dangerous, unstable), optical replacements with low-light imaging, subdermal armour patches (crude, painful), stimulant injectors, biometric spoofing patches. Failure rates are non-trivial.

**Surveillance:** Sub-Districts 1–6 have corporate drone coverage on randomised schedules. Sub-Districts 7–9 have intermittent blind spots exploited by gangs — and in this scenario, by whoever has been hiding the entity's spread.

### Law and Order
No city police force. Public security is contracted to Meridian Security Group, covering Sub-Districts 1–4. Sub-Districts 5–9 have periodic patrol only. The Undertow is unpatrolled. Crimes in Sub-Districts 7–9 are filed as statistics. Forty-seven disappearances barely rated a quarterly report.

### The Undertow (Sub-Districts 7–9)
The decayed industrial skeleton of old Neo-Ashford: processing plants, water infrastructure, manufacturing facilities, and the maintenance tunnels connecting them. Most decommissioned in the 2050s–60s when arcology construction made centralised industrial sites obsolete. Sub-District 7 has a surface population of approximately 40,000 — shift workers, black market vendors, people who couldn't or wouldn't go further up the social ladder. Sub-Districts 8 and 9 are largely abandoned at surface level.

### Veltris Solutions
Mid-tier corporate entity, 340,000 employees, primary focus: data logistics, supply-chain analytics, corporate intelligence. Revenue 2086: approximately 4.2 billion credits. Not powerful enough for the eight-corporation policy table, but powerful enough to operate in grey areas. Their legal team is renowned. Their black-ops division is not officially acknowledged.

### The Smell
Players should notice the smell before they see anything. A faint organic sweetness — not unpleasant exactly, but wrong for an industrial drainage environment. Like warm rain on cut grass, but with something beneath it that does not belong. It is strongest near drain grates and maintenance access hatches. Residents of Sub-District 7 have stopped noticing it. Newcomers will.

## Running Neo-Ashford — GM World Texture Guide
*Reference for how to describe this world during play. Do not read aloud — use as a source of language and sensory detail.*

### What Things Cost
Credits are the universal currency, transacted via wrist chip. Physical cash (printed credit cards) is used when anonymity matters — which is often in Sub-Districts 7–9.

| Item | Cost | Context |
|------|------|---------|
| Cheap meal (noodles, protein pack) | 3–5 credits | Daily food for most Undertow residents |
| Drink at the Terminus Bar | 8–12 credits | Jed pours heavy |
| Night in a sub-district room | 40–60 credits | Reth paid a week upfront: 280 credits |
| Black-market augment (basic) | 800–2,000 credits | Reflex tap, optical swap |
| Corporate-grade augment | 8,000–25,000 credits | Same range as tonight's payment |
| **8,000 credits (the payment)** | — | **Three months' wages for a skilled Undertow worker. A year's rent. Enough to move up one sub-district.** |

*The 8,000 credits is not life-changing money for corporate operatives. It is enormous money for Sub-District 7. This contrast matters when the moral choice arrives.*

### What People Wear
**Corporate zone:** Structured coats, clean materials, no visible augments (they are subtle and flush), dry clothes — they rarely go outside.
**Undertow:** Layered, practical, worn. Filter scarves. Boots. Street augments visible and functional, not decorative. Everyone carries something in a pocket that shouldn't be visible.
**The team:** Functional, not corporate. They look like people who have been in both worlds and belong completely to neither.

### How People Talk
The Undertow is clipped, watchful, economical. People trade information; they do not volunteer it. Jed Osler is the canonical example: everything they say is true; nothing is said that wasn't asked for.

Corporate speech is precise, modulated, and complete. Sable Morn speaks in full sentences with considered pauses and means everything including the things she does not say.

**Useful phrases:**
- *"Below street"* — in the tunnels or drainage infrastructure
- *"Went down and didn't come back clean"* — local idiom for someone changed by the entity
- *"Corporate side"* — working within arcology culture; implies moral compromise
- *"Chip it"* — pay digitally
- *"Run dark"* — operate without electronic trace, cash only, no transit-chip pings

### Sensory Language by Location
Use one or two per location. More becomes a lecture.

**Corporate briefing suite:** Processed air. Polymer carpet that absorbs footsteps. Manufactured warmth. Everything grey and precisely lit. The windows show the city like a photograph.

**The Slab:** Noise first. Voices, pneumatic tools, something frying in oil. Orange neon catching rain-slick on the road surface. The faint, sweet wrongness rising from the drain grates.

**The Terminus Bar:** Low-light amber. Humidity of people who have been somewhere all day. Beer and something astringent. Booths absorbing conversation. Jed's servo arm has a quiet rhythmic whine when they move.

**The tunnels:** Warmth increasing as they descend. Water somewhere always. The organic sweetness stronger — by The Junction, inescapable. Bioluminescence not enough to see by but enough to know you can see it.

**Sub-Level 9 / Sutter-Vane:** Industrial cold replaced by the entity's warmth. The sweetness thick enough to taste. A silence that is full — occupied by the quality of something very large, very slow, very patient.

### What Technology Looks Like
Screens are projected, curved, surface-embedded. Hacking is tactile — the Tech's suite physically interfaces with access points. Drones are palm-sized or smaller; the Tech's is silent-running. The bio-scanner is a wrist-strap device that gives text readouts. When it reads "MEMETIC HAZARD," the text is yellow on grey. Corporate equipment uses yellow for warnings. Yellow is worse than red in some ways — it means the system knows what it is looking at and is not willing to say.

## Introduction *(~15 minutes)*

### Opening — Read Aloud or Paraphrase

> The elevator doesn't announce its floor. It just opens, and you're in a reception suite that smells of processed air and expensive polymer. The carpet absorbs sound. Everything is grey and clean. Outside the floor-to-ceiling windows, Neo-Ashford glitters in the acid haze forty storeys below — the Undertow's amber and red neon faint beneath you, the arcology towers rising on all sides like the walls of a machine you're standing inside.
>
> The woman waiting for you doesn't stand up. Late forties, expensive coat. Corporate augments visible as a faint ridge along her jaw — subdermal comms. She looks at the wall slightly to your left when she speaks, as if watching something you can't see.
>
> "Sit down," she says. "There's a job."

### Player Briefing
Sable Morn provides the mission briefing verbally, supported by handouts 91-H00-A and B:

- Damos Reth, a Veltris data analyst, has gone missing in Sub-Districts 7–9
- He was carrying a proprietary data-chip. The chip is the priority; Reth is secondary
- Payment: 8,000 credits each on completion. Half now, half on delivery
- The NDA covers everything they see during the operation
- Their extraction window is 48 hours

**What Sable will NOT say:** Why Reth left. That a second Veltris team is already in the tunnels. That the NDA's biological material clause means anything. That she has already authorised their termination if they surface with the full story.

`[BRP: Research or Law 30% — notices the biological material clause in the NDA. Sable, if asked, dismisses it: "Standard boilerplate from a separate division. Don't worry about it." She does not look worried. She does not look anything.]`

`[BRP: Insight 40% — Sable is not telling them something, and the gap where it should be is quite large. She is not lying; she is simply not offering.]`

### Character Introductions
After the briefing, give players two minutes each: how they carry themselves in this room, what they notice about Sable Morn, and whether the money feels right. The Fixer will have the most considered response to Sable. The Ghost is already mentally back in Sub-District 7.

### Atmosphere Notes
- Music: Vangelis-style ambient during the briefing. Shift to low industrial as they descend.
- Sable Morn: deliberate stillness. She doesn't fidget. She doesn't reassure.
- The briefing room is one notch too clean. It feels prepared — like a set dressed for this specific meeting.
- Let players ask questions. Answer factually and minimally. If asked why Reth ran: a pause, then "Company matters."

## ACT ONE — The Job *(~55 minutes)*
> **Goal:** Establish the Undertow. Plant the seeds of wrongness. Give the team the essential tools (Reth's map, first evidence of the entity) and pull them deeper.

### Summary
The team descends into Sub-District 7. Routine fieldwork at first — buying information, following Reth's trail, navigating the texture of the Undertow. Then they reach Reth's room and see the photograph. After that, nothing feels quite as manageable.

### Location: The Slab

**Description:** The main elevated transit artery into Sub-District 7. A covered road from the 2040s, now encrusted with semi-permanent stalls, wire rigging, cargo containers repurposed as storefronts. Always crowded in evening hours. Always watched.

**Atmosphere:** Noise, heat, synthesised food and something oily. Neon from the stalls catches the rain slick on the road surface, turns it orange and pink. And underneath it all — faint, sweet, wrong — the smell. Rising from the drain grates in the walkway. Nobody else seems to notice it.

**Key Details:**
- Information can be bought here. `[BRP: Persuade or Fast Talk — a mid-level vendor confirms Reth was spotted two nights ago, heading deeper into Sub-District 7. "Scared. Paying cash. Not talking to anyone." Costs 50 credits.]`
- The Rivet Teeth gang controls the east section. Three or four members visible. They are watching but will NOT initiate unless provoked. Leader Scratch emerges if approached. `[BRP: Insight 40% — she's cautious, not hostile. Knows something happened in the sub-levels. Won't say what unless treated as a person worth talking to.]` *(RED HERRING — not connected to the entity)*
- `[BRP: Spot or Electronics 50% — notices drone patrol routes have been rerouted, carving blind spots in a corridor pointing toward Sub-District 9. Someone with corporate clearance has been doing this for months.]`
- **The child:** Approaches The Tech specifically, presses a folded note into their hand, vanishes into the crowd. The note: *Don't go to the plant. My dad went. He didn't come back the same.* The paper has a faint chemical smell the team will later recognise. The child is approximately nine. Gone before anyone can follow.

`[BRP: Track 40% — the child lives in a stairwell above a food vendor, 200 metres east. They will say nothing more. Their father's name is Gavrel Muss. He disappeared three weeks ago, returned for one day, then walked back below street at 3 a.m. and did not return.]`

**[ESSENTIAL CLUE — #1]** The surveillance blind spots. Someone corporate is hiding the entity's footprint from above.

### Location: The Terminus Bar

**Description:** A low-ceilinged bar at the far end of Sub-District 7, accessed by a narrow staircase from the street. The sign outside is broken — just the word BAR in amber. Regulars know it.

**Atmosphere:** Dim, warm, slightly humid. Strip lighting above a long bar. Six booths along the far wall. The clientele are shift workers, people who live upstairs, people who don't want to be found. Nobody looks up when the team walks in, which takes effort.

**Jed Osler is behind the bar.** Prosthetic left arm (old hydraulic model — chunky segmented fingers, audible servo whine on grip and release), non-binary, 50s, the stillness of someone who has seen everything and chosen not to be bothered by most of it.

`[BRP: Insight 40% — Jed is sizing the team up from the moment they walk in. Friendly, direct, patient, cash if appropriate — opens them up. Flash the Veltris logo or threaten — shut down entirely, permanently.]`

**What Jed will say, if treated well:**
- Reth was here four nights ago. Nervous, paid cash, ordered two drinks and didn't finish either
- Asking about "the disappearances" from the surrounding blocks
- Paid for a week upstairs, room 4. Has not been back since. "Didn't take his bag."
- The organic smell started "maybe a year ago. Maybe longer. You stop smelling it eventually."
- The Ghost gets an automatic warm reception — the warmth is in the poured drink, not the words
- Will NOT go below street level for any price. When asked why: "People who go below street level around here don't always come back the same. And some of them come back and then go back down on their own. That's worse."

**[ESSENTIAL CLUE — #2]** Confirms Reth's presence, points to his room, anchors the disappearances and the smell as a connected pattern.

### Location: Reth's Squat (Room 4, Above the Bar)

**Description:** A rented room with a pull-down cot and a single overhead light. Chaos left by someone frightened and thinking fast. Scattered data-chips (all blank — wiped). Handwritten notes on paper. A corkboard on the wall.

**Atmosphere:** Stale air and adrenaline-sweat. The scent of someone who has been very afraid in a small space and couldn't leave it.

`[BRP: Spot 40% — the paper notes are torn where someone pressed too hard with the pen. The handwriting starts neat and becomes erratic toward the later pages.]`

**The handwritten notes [Handout 91-H01-A] contain:**
- A partial log beginning six days ago
- References to "SV-9 sub-level files" and "containment protocols" and "the 47"
- The phrase *it knows where the warmth is*, written once neatly, then repeated urgently three more times
- A sketched diagram of a branching organic structure (means nothing now; will mean something later)
- Near the bottom of the final page, smaller: *There is a second one. Sub-District 4. Two kilometres north. NOBODY KNOWS.*

**The corkboard:** A hand-drawn map of Sub-District 9 with drainage tunnel routes marked. One route highlighted in red. At the end of the red route: a small X and the words *MW pod 7*.

`[BRP: Research or Insight 40% — the map was drawn from Veltris infrastructure plans. Reth pulled this before he ran. He knew exactly where he was going.]`

**The photograph [Handout 91-H01-B — SANITY CHECK]:**
Pinned to the bottom of the corkboard. A photograph taken in a drainage tunnel. Behind the apparent subject, something is visible. The shape is large. It does not resolve into anything recognisable regardless of how long you look. Not machinery, not debris, not an animal. The more you look, the less it resembles anything your mind has a category for. The impression that it has moved — is still moving — is very difficult to dismiss.

`[BRP: Sanity 0/1 — seeing the photograph. The 1-point loss is the dawning certainty that the shape does not belong in the world you thought you were in.]`

`[BRP: Photography or Art 40% — the photograph has not been altered. Whatever is in it was there when the picture was taken.]`

**[ESSENTIAL CLUE — #3]** The corkboard map. The red route to Maintenance Wash Pod 7 is the navigational key. Without this, the team moves blind in Sub-Level 9.

**[OPTIONAL CLUE]** The photograph. SAN trigger and physical proof. Players who take it will find it matters at The Junction.

## ACT TWO — The Undertow *(~65 minutes)*
> **Goal:** Escalate the horror from atmospheric to visceral. Locate Reth. Reveal the entity's reality. Force the decision to ascend.

### Summary
Following Reth's map, the team descends into Sub-Level infrastructure — old drainage channels, maintenance shafts, the buried skeleton of the original city. The organic filament network appears first as an oddity, then as something deeply wrong. The second Veltris team is a genuine threat and time cost. Reth is found alive, traumatised, and in possession of answers that the team did not want.

---

### Location: The Entry Shaft

**Description:** The access hatch — heavy industrial steel, electronic lock — is where Reth's map indicates. The lock has been forced recently and badly repaired.

`[BRP: Electronics or Lockpick 40% — the bypassing was done with a high-grade corporate hacking suite. This was Veltris black ops. Not Reth.]`

**Atmosphere:** Descending the access ladder, the temperature rises slightly. The organic sweetness is immediately stronger. Comms carry faint interference — rhythmic, almost like a carrier wave, but not quite. Not a signal. A pattern.

---

### Location: The Junction

**Description:** A large circular drainage hub where six tunnels converge, approximately 400 metres below street level. Old concrete, rusted iron supports, standing water at the channel edges. The walls, ceiling, and upper sections of the support columns are covered in a pale filamentous growth — organic, faintly luminescent, warm to the touch.

**Atmosphere:** The bioluminescence gives no useful light but creates the impression of being inside something alive. The warmth is unmistakably biological. The smell is strong enough to be disorienting for the first few minutes. The silence is not empty — it has a quality, as if the space is occupied by attention.

`[BRP: Science (Biology) or Medicine 40% — not fungal, not bacterial, not any known taxonomy. The bio-scanner reads: 'organic: living: class: unclassified.' Sustained analysis returns: 'organic: living: class: unclassified: WARNING — POSSIBLE MEMETIC HAZARD.' The yellow text is very steady.]`

`[BRP: Sanity 1/1D6 — first sight of the filament growth. The loss comes from the quiet certainty that this is not how tunnels look, and that whatever made it has been here a very long time.]`

### The Moral Choice
*Present this at the ascent point. Do not telegraph a preferred outcome. All three are genuinely bad.*

**Option 1 — Walk Away**
Take the money. Sign the NDA. Return the chip to Sable Morn. Say nothing. Veltris continues the programme. The entity remains imperfectly contained — based on current growth rate, it will breach the final barriers in approximately six months. The secondary node in Sub-District 4 will have established itself by then.
*What it costs:* The team knows. They will always know.

**Option 2 — Expose Veltris**
Use Reth's data to go public or to a rival corporation. Veltris takes the damage — the programme is shut down, the plant is raided. The containment systems, with no one maintaining them, go offline over the following weeks. The entity, with no barriers and no observation, begins its slow spread again.
*What it costs:* The corporation is damaged. The entity is released. Nobody in the new oversight structure knows what they are dealing with.

**Option 3 — Destroy the Plant**
The Tech can trigger a catastrophic overload of the plant's power cells. Time to rig: ten minutes. During those ten minutes, power is rerouted from the remaining barriers — they fail within thirty seconds. The entity has ten minutes of freedom in Sub-Level 9. It cannot reach the upper floors in that time. The explosion destroys the primary node. The filament network withers over several weeks. The secondary node in Sub-District 4 is not affected.
*What it costs:* Power, water, heating, and medical equipment to the surrounding blocks goes out. The team can calculate the civilian casualties before they decide. Estimated between 15 and 30. Four of them will be children.

**If players attempt to combine options:** The mathematics are the answer. Ten minutes to rig plus Reth's condition plus the firefight upstairs exceeds what any timeline permits. They can do two of the three things. Not all three.

The flag is noticed by exactly one person — the Analyst, if they survived — because the flag contains a data signature that matches something buried in the files from The Watching Room.

Forty-eight hours later, the notification has been quietly deleted from their terminal by a process they did not initiate.

The next scenario is already beginning.

## GM Notes — Running The Night Crawler

### The Tone Problem (And How To Avoid It)
This is not a monster hunt. The scenario works by slow accumulation — the smell, the photograph, the growth, Reth's hands, Dr Chen's typing rhythm. The entity should not feel like a dungeon boss. It should feel like something that has always been there, which is worse.

The mistake to avoid: rushing the horror. Act One is the most important act for atmosphere. Spend time in the Terminus Bar. Let the Slab breathe. Let players sit with the photograph. The dread in Act Two is earned by Act One's normalcy.

The entity never speaks, never threatens, never chases in a straight line. It is simply present. The horror is accumulative and quiet. When it moves on the drone feed, it has already gone when the players look. The rhythm in the comms sounds almost like a name, but not quite.

### The Moral Choice — Do Not Help Them
When the team reaches the ascent and the three options are on the table, do not telegraph a preferred outcome. Present all three with equal weight and step back. Players will often try to find a fourth option — be open to creative solutions, but know that there is no clean answer. The entity has been here for centuries. Whatever they do tonight is a response to that, not a solution.

If players attempt all three (save Reth AND expose AND destroy): the timing mathematics don't work. Ten minutes to rig the overload plus time to get Reth out plus a firefight upstairs exceeds what the remaining barriers can hold. Something has to give.

### Sable Morn — The Delayed Threat
Sable is not a combat encounter during the session — she is a consequence in the epilogue. If the team surfaces with the data-chip and a public-exposure plan, she implements the termination order after they leave. Don't forget she exists in the briefing scene. Let players read the NDA. Let them notice she doesn't quite meet anyone's eyes. These details pay off.

### Dr Chen — Do Not Skip Her
The Observation Gallery is technically optional (the team can bypass to the stairwell). Don't let them skip it unless very short on time. Dr Chen is the most disturbing NPC precisely because she is not monstrous. She is a brilliant scientist who has been remade slowly by something patient, and she is not unhappy about it. The horror of her is gradual and consensual. This is worse than the alternatives.

**One practical technique for playing Dr Chen:** When speaking as her, look at the player slightly past their shoulder, not at their face. Hold it a beat longer than comfortable. Don't make it theatrical. Just hold it.

### Pacing Across Six Players
Three acts, six players, 3.5 hours = approximately eight minutes per player per act for direct engagement. Distribute attention actively:
- The Tech's drone gives a constant thread for quieter players
- The Medic's bio-scanner provides environmental information that rewards engagement
- The Analyst should be first to attempt decryption of the Veltris files — this is their moment
- The Ghost's Sub-District 7 background matters most in the Terminus Bar and with Reth
- The Fixer's Veltris history matters most in the briefing and at the moral choice

### The Child on The Slab
The child's father is Gavrel Muss — one of the people who went below street level, came back changed, and returned voluntarily to the plant. The child does not know their father is gone, or knows it and cannot process it. The note is their last attempt to do something useful in a situation entirely beyond them. Do not make this explicit during the session. Let it be what it is. If a player asks about the child after the session ends — that is when you tell them.

### What Happens If the Team Fights Everyone
Full combat mode — fights Kov and Sera, kills Dr Chen, takes out security upstairs — is a valid path. The entity does not care about the violence. The tunnels fill with noise and blood and the filaments contract toward warmth, but the primary node remains patient. Killing humans is not the solution. The scenario should make this clear without punishing players for fighting. Combat costs time and SAN, but it does not derail the narrative.

### The Three Bodies
Do not describe this graphically. The horror of the opened ribcages is in the mechanism, not the gore. They were unsealed. Something exited them from the inside. It took its time. The clinical precision of that description is worse than any amount of viscera. If players ask about the cause of death, let the Medic figure it out with the skill roll and then sit with it for a moment.
