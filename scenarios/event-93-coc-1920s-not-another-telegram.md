## Keeper's Overview

**"Not Another Telegram"** is a satirical-yet-genuinely-terrifying one-shot set in New York City, 1924. It wears the tropes of the genre like a well-tailored (if slightly blood-stained) overcoat — the investigators receive a mysterious telegram, visit a jazz club, find a corpse, consult a mad professor, discover an ancient tome, and stare into the abyss. The abyss, as usual, stares back.

**Tone:** Fawlty Towers meets *At the Mountains of Madness* — funny right up until it's absolutely horrifying. The self-aware humour should come from situation and NPC reaction, not from the Keeper winking at the camera. The comedy works because the world treats the absurdity as normal. The horror works because underneath that normalcy, something is genuinely wrong and getting worse. **Never let the players feel safe just because they're laughing.**

**The Conceit:** The scenario is called *Not Another Telegram* because it is literally another telegram — and the investigators know it. Experienced CoC players can lean into this. The opening NPC (Clarence Fitch, bellhop) can mutter "Oh, not another one of those" as he hands over the envelope. One investigator may groan aloud. This comedic self-awareness should dissolve within twenty minutes into genuine dread, as the scenario earns its horror.

**Design Philosophy:** The scenario deliberately rewards talking over fighting. The Herald is best resolved through Henderson. The Watcher is best resolved through the ritual. Investigators who try to shoot their way through will find it deeply unsatisfying and somewhat expensive in Sanity points.

## The Premise (Keeper Eyes Only)

In 1924, a mediocre occultist named **Reginald Ashworth** attempted to summon a servitor of Nyarlathotep during a private gathering at his Manhattan brownstone. He failed — almost. He didn't summon it fully; he *invited it in.* The entity — a **Skinless Watcher** — partially manifested and now haunts a trail of bodies across three locations in New York, pulling itself further into the physical world with each soul it consumes.

Ashworth is dead — the first victim — but he sent a telegram to his old friend and fellow dabbler **Professor Edna Whitmore** before he died. Whitmore has gone missing. The investigators are connected to Whitmore (see hooks below) and have each received a copy of the same cryptic telegram forwarded by her secretary.

**The telegram reads:**

> EDNA STOP THE WHEEL IS TURNING STOP DO NOT COME TO THE BROWNSTONE STOP THE SKIN DOES NOT FIT ANYMORE STOP REGINALD STOP

The investigators must piece together what happened, locate Whitmore, and find a way to banish the Skinless Watcher before it fully manifests — an event that will occur at **midnight of the same day.**

**Timeline:** The scenario begins at approximately 8:00 PM. Midnight is 4 hours away.

## Scene-by-Scene Breakdown

### INTRODUCTION — The Telegram Arrives (15 minutes)

**Setting:** The offices of Miss Hazel Pruitt, 3rd floor, 44 West 53rd Street. A small, efficient room. Filing cabinets. The smell of cigarettes and strong tea. A window looking down onto the street.

**Miss Hazel Pruitt:** 55, iron-grey bun, reading glasses on a chain, perpetual cigarette. Has worked for eccentric academics for twenty years and has learned to simply get on with it.

> *"Professor Whitmore received this four days ago. She packed a bag and left immediately. She told me to 'contact everyone on the list' if she hadn't returned by Friday. It is Friday."*

**Comedy beat:** The telegram says "DO NOT COME TO THE BROWNSTONE." Miss Pruitt confirms Whitmore went directly to the brownstone. Mickey (the Private Eye) may point out this is objectively very stupid.

**Second note:** Whitmore left a sealed envelope marked "OPEN IF THINGS GO WRONG." It reads:

> *"If things go wrong, talk to Delia at the Blue Orchid. She'll know what Reggie was into."*

**Blue Orchid details:** Basement speakeasy, 133rd Street, Harlem. Entry through a laundry. Password this week: **"Sassafras."** Open from 9 PM. Delia Monroe is the lounge singer.

---

### ACT ONE — Hot Jazz and Cold Leads (45 minutes)

#### The Blue Orchid Jazz Club

*133rd Street, Harlem. Basement, entry through a laundry. Password: Sassafras.*

The Blue Orchid is genuinely wonderful — warm amber lighting, cigarette smoke thick as wool, a six-piece band playing something that makes feet move before the brain approves. **Spend two minutes painting this scene.** It is the last comfortable place the investigators will be.

---

**Key NPC: Delia "Diamond" Monroe**

Lounge singer, 34. Ashworth's former lover. Black American woman of formidable intelligence and zero patience for nonsense. Emerald earrings. She is not a victim. She is not a villain. She is the most competent person in the scenario.

Getting Delia to talk: **Charm, Fast Talk, or Persuade** roll — or Lucky gets an **automatic success** through his music-world connections. Intimidate closes her down entirely.

**What Delia Knows:**
1. Ashworth was hosting occult "salons" at his brownstone on East 64th Street
2. It started after he found something at an estate sale — **a book wrapped in oilcloth**
3. Three weeks ago, guests started disappearing. One man found dead in his bathtub, skin described as "curiously loose."
4. Ashworth became increasingly erratic: *"It keeps looking at me from behind my own face."*
5. Prof. Whitmore came to the club three days ago. Delia told her to stay away from the brownstone. **Whitmore thanked her and went.**
6. Delia attended one early salon. Left after an hour because something felt wrong. Has never told anyone.

**Handout #1 — The Newspaper Clipping** *(Delia gives reluctantly)*

> *SOCIETY MAN FOUND DEAD IN EAST SIDE HOME — Reginald Ashworth, 41, socialite and amateur antiquarian, was found deceased at his residence on East 64th Street. Police report no signs of struggle. Coroner describes cause of death as 'undetermined cardiac event.' Mr. Ashworth is survived by no immediate family. His household staff resigned en masse the following morning and could not be reached for comment.*
> — New York Evening Post, six days ago

---

**The Herald at the Blue Orchid**

As the investigators prepare to leave, the band stumbles — one odd, wrong note.

- **Lucky (Music roll):** On success, recognises this wasn't a mistake. It was intentional. A signal.
- **Any investigator (Spot Hidden):** A thin man in a grey suit near the bar leaves before he can be confronted.

This is the **Herald** — Henderson's body, worn by the Watcher. He reappears at the Brownstone.

If investigators give chase: The Herald moves with wrong-footed grace. If cornered, he uses the Stare attack (POW vs POW — SAN 0/1D3 on failure) and slips away. He does not speak here.

### ACT TWO — The Brownstone on East 64th (60 minutes)

*A handsome four-storey townhouse. Dark windows. Brass knocker: an eye. The front door is unlocked.*

This is the first moment where comedy shifts to unease. **Why is it unlocked?**

---

**The Drawing Room (15 minutes)**

Dust sheets over furniture. Expensive portraits on dark walls.

- **Spot Hidden:** In every portrait, the subject's face has been carefully cut out — framed bodies with oval voids where faces should be.
- **Doctor:** The cuts are surgical.
- **Academic:** Recognises two empty frames — notable New York socialites, both recently reported missing.

**Comedy beat (do not skip):** One portrait is of a dog. Its face has also been cut out. Nobody can explain why this is funnier and sadder than the human ones. The dog portrait quietly ends up with the Reporter at the end of the scenario.

---

**The Library — First Floor (25 minutes)**

**Handout #2 — Ashworth's Journal** *(leather-bound, on the desk)*

> *Oct 4th — The book arrived today. I am not sure what language it is. I am not sure it is a language.*
>
> *Oct 19th — I read aloud from page 47. Nothing happened. Or rather, nothing happened that I could see.*
>
> *Nov 2nd — There is something behind my reflection now. Not in the mirror. Behind it. In the space where the reflection lives.*
>
> *Nov 14th — The salons are going splendidly. Everyone is fascinated. I am beginning to understand why it wants faces.*
>
> *Nov 29th — I think I invited it in. I cannot close the door. The threshold is midnight — that is the only wall left between partial and full manifestation. I have written to Edna. I hope she does not come here.*
>
> *[Final entry, four days ago:] IT IS WEARING HENDERSON NOW. HENDERSON VISITED TUESDAY. I AM SO SORRY.*

---

**Handout #3 — The Faceless Codex** *(on a lectern, wrapped in oilcloth)*

Small, bound in something not quite leather. Pages feel subtly wrong — slightly warm.

| Roll | Result |
|------|--------|
| Library Use (Academic/Doctor) | Script resembles pre-Columbian Mesoamerican glyphic writing — centuries old |
| Study it — SAN roll (1/1D6) — Success | Understand nothing but feel profoundly watched |
| Study it — SAN roll (1/1D6) — Fail | Hear in the mind: *"The Face is a door. The door can be opened from either side."* |

The Codex is the ritual text needed for the banishment. It does not need to be fully read — it needs to be *present and spoken from* during the ritual.

---

**The Basement — Whitmore Found (20 minutes)**

Chalk circle on the stone floor, mostly smeared. Candles burned to nothing. **Professor Edna Whitmore**, alive, tied to a chair, blindfolded, earplugs in — having done this to herself with trembling hands.

When the blindfold is removed, she grabs the nearest person by the lapels:

> *"Don't let it see your face."*

**What Whitmore Knows:**
1. The Skinless Watcher is a servitor entity — not a Great Old One, but a piece of one, sent ahead as a scout
2. It cannot exist fully in the physical world without a face — a human face worn like a mask feeds it enough energy to solidify
3. Ashworth tried to close the ritual. He couldn't. He became its first face.
4. The ritual circle in the basement almost worked. She was trying to complete it when she heard the entity moving upstairs.
5. **The banishment requires:**
   - The Codex (they have it)
   - The original summoning point (this room)
   - One investigator standing before the entity holding a mirror, not looking away, while the others complete the incantation

---

**The Herald Returns (end of Act Two)**

Sound from upstairs. The thin man in grey is in the drawing room. He has followed them.

Face moves slightly too much — like a mask settling. Eyes are Ashworth's. Hands are his own.

**Dark comedy beat:** The Herald knocks over a vase coming down the stairs. He apologises, reflexively. He is still, somewhere inside, Mr. Henderson. The investigators will laugh nervously and then feel terrible.

**Henderson's details:** Mr. Charles Henderson, 44, insurance clerk. Wife: Margaret. He attended Ashworth's final salon because Ashworth owed him money and the party seemed a good opportunity to raise the subject.

**Resolving the Herald:**
- Combat is possible but draws the Watcher sooner
- **Psychology roll:** Investigators notice something human straining underneath the wrongness
- The Herald can speak with Ashworth's voice (SAN 0/1D4 — it knows things only Ashworth knew)
- Best resolution: persuade Henderson, save him for Act Three's crucial moment

---

### ACT THREE — The Watcher in the Glass (55 minutes)

#### Scene 3-1: Completing the Circle (20 minutes)

Whitmore needs investigators' help to restore the chalk ritual circle.

| Task | Mechanic | Outcome if Failed |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| Understand the pattern | Occult roll | Whitmore guides — costs 5 minutes |
| Draw the circle | Art/Craft (Drawing) or DEX | One section must be redrawn — costs 5 min + 0/1 SAN |
| Hold off the Herald | Combat, Persuade, or positioning | He disrupts — adds 1D3 rounds to incantation |

**Sanity pressure:** The Herald can speak with Ashworth's voice from behind the wrong face. SAN 0/1D4 for investigators who react to the voice. He says things only Ashworth would know. He begs for help. He may be genuinely in there.

---

#### Scene 3-2: The Watcher Arrives (35 minutes)

**Read aloud:**

> *"The temperature drops first — not to cold, but to wrong. The shadows in the corner of the room stop behaving. Then you see it, and your brain performs a small, polite act of mercy by refusing to fully process what it is. It is tall. It is not quite standing. It has faces — several of them, worn one atop the other like pages in a book. The outermost is recent. You recognise it as Reginald Ashworth from his newspaper photograph. He is smiling. He has always been smiling. He was smiling before his face was removed. The thing beneath the faces has no features of its own. That is the worst part. It is looking at you with nothing. And you feel, with horrible certainty, that it is measuring your face. Checking the fit."*

---

**Phase 1 — The Offering of Reflection**

One investigator volunteers. They stand before the Watcher holding the gilt-framed oval mirror from the wall.

- SAN roll: **1D6/1D20**
- Look away → Watcher lunges
- Hold → Watcher is transfixed (sees itself with no face) — paralysed for the duration of the incantation

**Best candidates:** Vivienne (the Heiress — social courage at its limit) or Finch (the Academic — intellectual reckoning made physical). Any investigator can step up. **Give whoever volunteers a moment of genuine weight.**

**If no one volunteers:** Whitmore does it herself — but she is weakened. She must make a Hard SAN roll. Failure breaks the paralysis early and the Watcher can act for 1D3 rounds.

---

**Phase 2 — The Incantation**

Whitmore leads three investigators reading from the Codex. Three consecutive **Occult rolls** (recommended: Hard difficulty).

- Failed roll: SAN damage **1/1D4** as the words do things to the reader's mouth that words should not do. The ritual still continues.
- Critical success on any roll: No SAN cost for that reader; reduces next roll's difficulty by one level.

**The incantation (read this aloud as investigators begin, then continue silently):**

> *"Whatever face it wears, it is not the face. Whatever door it opens, the door opens from both sides. We name ourselves. We claim the threshold. We give it nothing. We give it nothing. We give it nothing."*

*(The words repeat. They should not make the investigators feel powerful. They should feel like standing very still while something enormous passes.)*

---

**Phase 3 — Henderson's Choice**

As the ritual nears completion, the Herald becomes lucid for a final moment.

- **Psychology or Persuade roll** from an investigator — or pure roleplaying can grant it automatically
- If players speak to Henderson — really speak to him, not to the monster — the Keeper can simply grant it

> *Henderson goes still. His hands stop moving. He says, in his own voice:*
> *"Tell my wife the cat needs feeding on Thursdays. She always forgets."*
> *Then the ritual completes.*

**The Banishment:**

The Watcher collapses inward. Not violently — it simply stops fitting in the physical world and falls through the floor like water through paper. The faces it wore scatter across the stone. Ashworth's face, last of all, settles face-down on the chalk circle. The Herald's body crumples. The candles relight themselves, and immediately go out.

Silence.

> *"Well. That was about what I expected." — Professor Edna Whitmore*

Someone should laugh. Someone might cry. Both are correct.

## Keeper's Notes — Tone Management

### Comedy-to-Horror Transition Guide

| Phase | Tone Target | How to Achieve It |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Dry comedy, bureaucratic | Miss Pruitt's exhausted normalcy. The telegram says don't go there. She went there. |
| Act One | Warm and bittersweet | The Blue Orchid is genuinely lovely. Let the players enjoy it. The wrong note is the first crack. |
| Act Two (entering) | Unease beneath beauty | The brownstone is beautiful and very quiet and the door is unlocked. Take your time. |
| Act Two (library) | Curiosity mixed with dread | The journal. The Codex. The slowly dawning understanding. |
| Act Two (Herald) | Dark comedy turning sincere | The vase. The apology. The joke that stops being a joke. |
| Act Three | Horror with earned weight | Henderson's last line must land. Do not undercut it. |
| Epilogue | Quiet, slightly stunned relief | Whitmore's line. The final telegram. Cold hand on the neck. |

### Key Keeper Commitments

1. **Never let players feel safe just because they're laughing.** The comedy and the horror are the same thing viewed from different distances.
2. **The Herald is a tragedy.** Henderson is in there. The investigators can sense it. This is not a monster to be shot.
3. **The mirror scene gets weight.** Pause. Consider asking whoever volunteers to stand up from the table if they want to. Let the other players watch.
4. **Henderson's cat line is the emotional peak.** Do not rush past it. Let it land. Let there be silence.
5. **The final telegram lands like a cold hand.** End there. No explanation. No additional beats.

### If Investigators Try to Fight Their Way Through
- The Herald can be subdued but not meaningfully killed — Henderson's body will not stop until the ritual completes or fails
- The Watcher cannot be harmed by conventional weapons. 2 points of resistance + immune to fire.
- The ritual is the only resolution. Whitmore will make this clear.

### Midnight Pressure
- Don't count down mechanically — let it sit as background dread
- The brownstone has a grandfather clock in the drawing room — set it to **9:30 PM** when investigators arrive
- If Act Three runs long: *"We have perhaps forty minutes before midnight. I suggest we do not spend it discussing options."*

### What Happens at Midnight? (The Failure State)

This section exists because a GM running cold deserves to know.

**If midnight arrives before the ritual completes**, the Watcher fully manifests. The physical constraints no longer apply — mirror aversion fails, the chalk circle loses its power, the entity is no longer anchored to the basement. It is simply *here*, in full, and it is hungry.

Read aloud (failure):
> *"The grandfather clock in the drawing room above strikes twelve. You feel it as a physical thing — not a sound, a shift. A door opening, somewhere much deeper than the basement floor. The Watcher makes a sound. It is not a sound that belongs to any face. It turns, and something in its posture changes. It has been looking for faces. It stops looking. It begins taking."*

This is a total failure. There is no combat resolution. Investigators who do not flee immediately suffer the Face Peel attack. Those who flee have one last image: the basement window, viewed from the street, the light inside turning wrong colours, and then going out.

**In practice:** The scenario is structured so this should not happen. Whitmore is there and functional. The Codex is visible. The clues feed naturally to the ritual. If midnight arrives despite all of this, it means the players have genuinely lost — honour it. Don't soften it. End on: *"Somewhere in this city, a week later, a telegram is sent from no address. It reads: INTERESTING STOP WILL BE WATCHING STOP. There are others."*

### What If Investigators Destroy the Codex?

If investigators attempt to destroy the Codex before the banishment — which at least one investigator will attempt, probably the Academic who has read too much Lovecraft — Whitmore stops them.

*"You cannot burn it. I tried. The paper doesn't catch. Whatever it's bound in doesn't burn. Believe me, I would have done it by now."*

The Codex cannot be destroyed by fire or by conventional means. It can only be neutralised after the banishment, at which point Whitmore will take it and say she knows someone who can dispose of it properly. (She does not know anyone who can dispose of it properly. She will lock it in a bank vault in Vermont. This is fine for approximately thirty years.)

If investigators insist on destroying it *during* the ritual (mid-incantation) — they disrupt the banishment. The Watcher is freed from the mirror paralysis early. The circle must be re-established. This costs time and SAN (0/1D6 per investigator present as the Watcher turns its attention).
