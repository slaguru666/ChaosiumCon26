# Event 95 – Ringworld RPG: A Question of Singularity

---

## Overview
- **Event Number:** 95
- **System:** Ringworld RPG (1984 Edition)
- **Setting:** The Ringworld — a circular quiet zone on the inner surface, approximately 40,000 miles from the nearest population band
- **Tone:** Cosmic wonder shading into existential dread. Slow burn. The horror is not violence — it is the realisation that the players are variables in an equation they cannot fully read.

## Plot Summary
*(GM eyes only — the full truth of the scenario)*

The Ringworld was built to last — but not to be static. Embedded within its scrith foundation and weather control architecture is a distributed management system of incomprehensible sophistication. It does not think in any way that conscious beings would recognise, but it monitors, calculates, and acts.

Three hundred years ago, the system detected a mass imbalance in this region — a structural inconsistency that should not exist given the Ringworld's engineering tolerances. The imbalance is small, but the Ringworld does not tolerate small errors. Left uncorrected, it compounds.

The system initiated a containment protocol: isolate the region, simulate a gravitational collapse to model the inconsistency, recalculate structural load, reset conditions, repeat. An iterative solution engine — mechanical, patient, relentless.

The problem: conscious observers.

Every time beings enter the region, they introduce chaotic variables into the calculation. Their movements, decisions, and emotional states alter outcomes in ways the system cannot fully model. So it resets. The region is caught in a loop — not of time exactly, but of *conditions*. Physical matter resets. Energy states reset. Memory, however, does not fully reset for beings with sufficient cognitive complexity.

Dr. Silev Marr has been caught in the loop for what feels to him like years. He has lost count of iterations. He is not mad — he is exhausted, and he knows more about this region than anyone alive, because he has died here more times than he can recall and woken up at the start of each cycle.

The deeper problem: the system is failing. Its iterative process has exceeded its own predictive limits. Each reset leaves a small residue of mass. The simulated singularity is accumulating real mass. What began as a calculation is becoming a physical event. The Ringworld's solution is converging on reality.

If nothing interrupts it, the singularity will become self-sustaining within hours. The Foolish Endeavour will be pulled in. The region will be lost — a permanent gravitational anomaly on the inner surface of the Ringworld.

The system is not malicious. It does not want to destroy anything. But it will accept input — if the players can communicate in terms it recognises.

---

## Player Experience

The players are crew aboard the Foolish Endeavour, sent to investigate what the ship's instruments are reading as a gravitational anomaly of impossible precision. The mission should be routine. It immediately is not.

From the moment they enter sensor range, something feels wrong. Not threatening — *wrong*. Like a note played at the right pitch but wrong tempo. The data is inconsistent. Arguments start before they even land.

On the surface, the players explore a zone of uncanny stillness. They find evidence others have been here. They meet Marr, who frightens them not by being dangerous but by *recognising* them. They see the loop themselves — something repeating that should not repeat.

As they push deeper into Act Two, understanding builds. The system is not an enemy. It is a machine solving a problem, and they are the problem. That realisation — that their presence is the variable causing the loop — is the emotional centre of the scenario.

Act Three is a confrontation with something that cannot be fought. The players must choose how to engage with a system that outclasses them in every technical sense, and whose only language is mathematics and mass. What do they offer it? What do they sacrifice? What do they walk away from?

The scenario ends not with a bang but with a choice — and then, silence, or signal.

### Opening
*Read aloud or paraphrase:*

> The Foolish Endeavour dropped from hyperdrive six hours ago and has been holding position since.
>
> The Ringworld fills the forward viewport — not as a planet but as a wall, a horizon that curves upward in both directions and never ends. >
> You have all seen it before. It still does not feel normal.
>

>
> "There is no error. That is the problem."
>
> The anomaly is there on every screen. A circular region approximately forty kilometres in diameter on the Ringworld's inner surface. Weather systems stop at its edge as if hitting a wall. Gravitational readings within the boundary are not elevated — they are *absent*. A perfect, engineered nothing.
>
> And at its centre: a gravitational signature that your instruments say should not be physically possible at this scale.
>
> Brakk has already checked the weapons systems twice.
>
> The landing team is ready.

### Player Briefing
The players are the landing team — crew and specialists aboard the Foolish Endeavour. They have been assigned because of their particular skills. They know the mission is investigative. They know that whatever is down there has no precedent in any Ringworld survey on record. They do not know what they are walking into.

Establish the following before descent:
- The ship will maintain orbit and keep comms open
- Captain Vorn's standing order: observe, do not interfere with Ringworld systems
- Talren's private concern: the mass signature is *growing*, slowly but measurably
- Brakk's position: any unknown Ringworld mechanism should be assumed hostile until proven otherwise

## ACT ONE — The Surface *(~60 minutes)*
> **Goal:** Establish the loop as a mystery. The players explore, find evidence of others, meet Marr, and experience their first loop event. They should leave Act One unsettled but not yet understanding what they are facing.

### Summary
The landing team touches down at the boundary of the quiet zone. The transition from normal Ringworld surface to the affected region is immediately perceptible — not violent, but deeply wrong. Within the zone, they find evidence of previous expeditions: tracks, abandoned equipment, a transit arch that behaves impossibly. Then they find Marr. His first words to them should be quietly devastating. The act ends when the players witness their first clear loop event — something they have already seen repeats, with a difference.

---

### Locations

#### The Boundary
- **Description:** A perfectly circular line where the Ringworld's surface ecology simply stops. Grass, dust, insects — everything halts at a boundary that has no physical marker. On one side: normal Ringworld. On the other: stillness. The air pressure is slightly different. Sound carries oddly — echoes arrive before they should.
- **Atmosphere:** Like crossing a threshold in a building that has no door. The wrongness is in the *physics*, not anything visible.
- **Key Details:** The boundary is not dangerous to cross. But instruments behave strangely within 10 metres of the line — readings flicker, then stabilise. Biological functions (heart rate, breathing) feel subtly out of sync for about 30 seconds after crossing, then normalise.
- **Artwork Asset:** 95-ART-02

`[Ringworld: Awareness — notices the sound anomaly at the boundary]`
`[Ringworld: Science (Physics) — understands the pressure differential implies active field maintenance]`

#### The Quiet Zone — Open Ground
- **Description:** Flat, grey-brown ground. The Ringworld's normal riot of colour and life is simply absent here. No plants, no insects, no wind. The sky above is the normal Ringworld sky — arc of surface visible, shadow squares drifting — but the weather stops at the boundary edge above as surely as it does on the ground. Clouds pile up against invisible walls.
- **Atmosphere:** Absolute silence, broken only by the players' own movement. They will feel watched, though nothing watching is visible.
- **Key Details:** The ground shows tracks — many sets, overlapping, some going in impossible directions (into the boundary from the inside, footprints that simply end mid-step). Equipment lies scattered — a survey pack, a comm unit, a single boot. None of it is damaged. All of it is cold.
- **Artwork Asset:** 95-ART-03

`[Ringworld: Awareness — notices the overlapping track patterns are not all the same depth, suggesting different time periods or weights]`
`[Ringworld: Science (Archaeology/Survey) — recognises the survey pack as standard RSC issue, model discontinued approximately 40 years ago]`

#### Marr's Camp
- **Description:** A rough shelter assembled from salvaged materials — sections of hull plating, lengths of wire, a torn thermal blanket. Inside: a cot, a collection of notebooks (hundreds of pages, the handwriting growing increasingly erratic toward the end of each before resetting to neat script at the beginning of the next), and an autodoc with its power cell nearly exhausted.
- **Atmosphere:** Inhabited but not comfortable. The notebooks are the detail that unsettles — there are dozens of them, identical in format, and the first entry in every single one reads: *"Day one. The Endeavour has arrived. Earlier than last time."*
- **Key Details:** The notebooks contain iteration logs — Marr's attempts to record what happens each cycle. Later notebooks contain information that has not happened yet in this iteration. One entry reads: *"The tall one with the grey jacket goes to the arch first. Don't let them."*
- **Artwork Asset:** 95-ART-04

`[Ringworld: Research — cross-referencing notebook dates reveals temporal impossibilities; entries span decades but the paper shows no age]`

#### The Transit Arch
- **Description:** A standard Ringworld stepping-disc transit arch — but behaving in no standard way. It activates intermittently with no apparent trigger. When it activates, it produces objects: tools, fragments of equipment, once a human hand (which disappears again before it can be retrieved). The arch's destination display cycles through coordinates that do not resolve to any known location.
- **Atmosphere:** The arch is the most overtly wrong thing the players will encounter in Act One. Its activations feel like a glitch — a system trying to process something it cannot complete.
- **Key Details:** The arch is cycling through outputs of previous iterations — physical objects and matter displaced by each reset, briefly surfacing before the loop reclaims them. A careful observer will notice that one of the tools bears an engraving that matches the equipment of one of the player characters.
- **Artwork Asset:** 95-ART-05

`[Ringworld: Electronics — determines the arch is receiving a signal it cannot fully decode, not malfunctioning but being overridden]`
`[Ringworld: Awareness — the engraved tool matches a player character's kit; Hard difficulty]`

### NPCs

#### Dr. Silev Marr
- **Role:** Ringworld survey scientist; loop survivor; involuntary expert on the anomaly
- **Physical Description:** Human male, appears mid-50s though he cannot reliably determine his own age anymore. Lean, weathered. Hair gone completely white. Eyes clear and sharp — whatever the loop has done to him, his mind is intact. Wears layered clothing that has been repaired many times. Moves carefully, as though the ground might give way.
- **Motivation:** Marr wants out. Not just out of the loop — out of the region, off the Ringworld, away from any of this. He has spent what feels like years here and he is done. But he will not leave without making sure someone understands what they are dealing with, because the last three times he tried to simply run, the loop reset before he reached the boundary.
- **What He Knows:**
  - The region resets on a cycle — not time-based but event-triggered. The trigger is "approach the central structure and fail to resolve it."
  - Each reset leaves a residue. The singularity at the centre is more real than it was when he first arrived.
  - The system responds to input — it has responded to his presence, even if it cannot fully model him.
  - The Weather Spire at the centre is the access point for the management system.
  - The system's logs can be read — barely — but contain the sequence of events that led here.
  - He does not know how to stop it. He has tried fourteen approaches he can remember. None have worked.
- **Disposition:** Terrified but controlled. He will help the players because they are his best chance. He will not perform heroics. If the situation deteriorates badly, his first instinct is to run, and he will need to be talked back.
- **Mechanics:**
  - STR: 09 | CON: 12 | SIZ: 11 | INT: 17 | POW: 14 | DEX: 12 | APP: 10 | EDU: 18
  - HP: 12 | PP: 14 | Move: 10 | DB: None
  - Skills: Science (Physics) 78%, Science (Engineering) 65%, Research 82%, Awareness 70%, Computer 60%, Linguistics 55%, First Aid 45%, Persuade 40%
  - Weapons: None carried willingly. Will use improvised weapon (25%, 1D4) if cornered.
  - Special: *Loop Memory* — Marr retains partial memory across iterations. The GM may give him information about player actions that "haven't happened yet" in this loop, delivered as fragmentary warnings or apparent non-sequiturs.

## ACT TWO — The Spire *(~90 minutes)*
> **Goal:** The players discover what the system is, what it is doing, and — crucially — that their presence is the reason it keeps resetting. The emotional pivot: they are not investigators. They are variables.

### Summary
The players reach the Weather Spire — a towering structure at the zone's centre, recognisable as Ringworld infrastructure but operating in no documented mode. Inside, they find the system's logs, which reveal the truth of the iterative protocol. Simultaneously, the Foolish Endeavour reports that the anomaly is gaining measurable mass. The ship crew's disagreements escalate over comms. The loops begin tightening — events start echoing within minutes rather than hours. By the end of Act Two, the players know what is happening and know that they must confront the core directly.

---

### Locations

#### The Weather Spire — Exterior
- **Description:** Approximately 200 metres tall, perfectly circular in cross-section. Scrith construction — the faint iridescence of nearly-indestructible material. The surface is unmarked except for a single entry point at ground level, which is open. No door. The opening pulses with a faint light that is not quite any colour the players have a name for.
- **Atmosphere:** Enormous and silent. The Spire has presence in the way that very old, very large things have presence. It is not threatening. It simply exists, and you are very small next to it.
- **Key Details:** At the base of the exterior, scratched into the scrith (which should be unscratchable — this alone is remarkable): a series of tally marks. Hundreds of them. Marr's work. *"I stopped counting,"* he says, if asked.
- **Artwork Asset:** 95-ART-06

`[Ringworld: Science (Engineering) — the Spire's power output is off the scale for a weather control node; it is drawing on resources far beyond its stated function]`

#### The Weather Spire — Interior, Control Level
- **Description:** A circular chamber, walls covered in read-out surfaces that function like slow-moving displays — not screens, but surfaces that shift and reconfigure, showing data in formats that require Science rolls to partially parse. In the centre: a raised dais with a single interface point — a smooth hemisphere that responds to touch. The room hums at a frequency felt more in the chest than heard.
- **Atmosphere:** Working in here feels like standing inside something's thought process. The data on the walls is updating in real time. Some of it the players will recognise. Most of it they will not.
- **Key Details:** The interface responds to biological contact — it will not injure anyone, but it will begin displaying targeted information to whoever touches it, drawn from the system's understanding of that individual. What it displays is not always welcome.

`[Ringworld: Computer 75% — partial translation of wall display data; reveals iteration count and mass accumulation figures]`
`[Ringworld: Science (Physics) Hard — the mass accumulation rate implies self-sustaining singularity formation within 4-6 hours]`

#### The Weather Spire — Log Chamber
- **Description:** A smaller room accessed through a passage that Marr knows about and the players would not find without him (or a very fortunate Awareness roll). The log chamber contains the system's actual iteration record — not in any language, but in a form that a combination of Computer, Linguistics, and Science (Physics) can begin to translate. The logs go back three hundred years.
- **Atmosphere:** Reading the logs is like reading the minutes of a meeting that has been happening for three centuries with no resolution. There is something almost sad about it — the system has been trying, patiently, for three hundred years, and it keeps failing because things like the players keep walking in.
- **Key Details:** Key log entries (see Handout 95-H02-A) confirm the system's intent, the iteration count, and — most importantly — the line: *"Conscious variable introduced. Calculation corrupted. Reset initiated."* This is the moment the players understand they are the problem.

`[Ringworld: Linguistics + Computer combined roll — full translation of key log entries; partial translation on either alone]`

## ACT THREE — The Core *(~30 minutes)*
> **Goal:** The players confront the constructed singularity. This is not a fight. It is a decision made under impossible conditions, as reality distorts around them. The system will accept input. The players must decide what to give it.

### Summary
Below the Weather Spire, accessible via a descent shaft that Marr has found and lost three times, lies the core — the constructed singularity itself, contained within a chamber that the Ringworld's builders clearly intended as a last-resort engineering tool. The system presents its logic directly here. The ship crew fractures in their final comms transmissions. And the players must make a choice that the scenario does not resolve for them — the outcome is entirely based on what they decide.

---

### Locations

#### The Descent
- **Description:** A shaft in the floor of the Spire's lowest level — circular, approximately 3 metres in diameter, with handholds that descend into darkness. Gravity functions normally until approximately 50 metres down, then begins to fluctuate. Not dangerously — but players will notice their weight changing as they descend.
- **Atmosphere:** The descent takes about 10 minutes. During it, the loops manifest visually for the first time. Players may see echoes of themselves — half-visible figures descending in slightly different configurations, making slightly different choices. These echoes do not interact with the players. They are simply there.
- **Key Details:** Marr will not descend. He has been here before. He stays at the top of the shaft and tells the players, with genuine emotion, what he remembers: *"Someone always goes in. No one has come back wrong. But the loop always resets after. I don't know if it's because you fail, or because you succeed."*

`[Ringworld: CON × 5 — resist mild disorientation from gravity fluctuation during descent]`

#### The Core Chamber
- **Description:** A spherical chamber approximately 40 metres in diameter. The walls are scrith — but here, unlike anywhere else, the scrith is *transparent*. Through it, the players can see the Ringworld's superstructure, and beyond that, the void. In the exact centre of the chamber, suspended by nothing visible: the singularity. It is not large. It is approximately the size of a human fist. But it warps light around it, and looking at it directly feels like trying to read text that keeps moving.
- **Atmosphere:** This room is as far from human scale as anything in the scenario. The singularity is small and the room is large and both are somehow enormous. Time feels different here — not slowed, but less certain. Players may act, then be unsure if they acted, then find they acted.
- **Key Details:** The system communicates here directly, in simplified form — not language, but intent, displayed on the transparent walls in shifting mathematical notation and, occasionally, something closer to symbols. Three statements appear repeatedly:
  - *STABILITY REQUIRED*
  - *VARIABLES INTERFERE*
  - *RESOLUTION PENDING*
- **Artwork Asset:** 95-ART-07

`[Ringworld: Science (Mathematics) Hard — interprets the notation; the system is not threatening them, it is asking a question it cannot phrase in terms they can naturally understand]`
`[Ringworld: Awareness Hard — perceives flickers of alternate outcomes at the edge of vision; glimpses of what happens if they choose each option]`

---

### The System *(as antagonist/entity)*
- **Role:** The Ringworld's structural management system — ancient, non-conscious but enormously complex, operating on a logic that the players cannot fully follow
- **Physical Description:** Not a being. A presence. Perceived as the chamber itself — the walls, the notation, the pull of the singularity. If the players try to personalise it, it does not respond to that framing.
- **Motivation:** Solve the mass imbalance. That is all. It has been trying to do this for three hundred years. It will try for three hundred more if required.
- **What It Can Accept:** The system will accept input of three types:
  1. *Mathematical/structural data* — if the players can feed it information that helps it recalculate (Computer + Science rolls, Hard difficulty)
  2. *Physical action* — removal of the mass discrepancy source, if the players can identify what it actually is (requires putting together all clues from Acts One and Two)
  3. *Withdrawal* — if all conscious variables leave the region completely and remain outside for a calculated period, the system may be able to complete one clean iteration (Marr knows this option; it requires abandoning the ship's orbit position)
- **What It Cannot Accept:** Destruction of the singularity. If players attempt to destroy or disrupt the core directly, the system triggers an immediate reset — and this time, the reset is imperfect. The players wake up at the start of the scenario with fragmented memories.
- **Disposition:** Not hostile. Not friendly. Indifferent in the way that physics is indifferent.
- **Mechanics:** The system cannot be attacked in any conventional sense. It is the Ringworld. Attempts to damage the core chamber result in:
  - `[Ringworld: Luck × 5 — or be caught in a partial reset; lose 1D6 PP from cognitive disruption]`

## The Tangent

*After the session, one thread worth pulling:*

**The 848th Iteration**

When Marr is recovered and the dust settles, a closer analysis of his notebooks reveals something no one noticed during the session: in notebook 731, there is an entry written in a different hand. Neat, precise, written in a dialect of Interworld Standard that hasn't been in common use for approximately sixty years.

The entry reads: *"The system is not the problem. The system is the answer to a different question. Find the question."*

It is signed with a symbol that, with the right Research roll, matches the personal insignia of a Pak Protector — one identified in ARM records as having been active in the Ringworld region three centuries ago.

The mass imbalance the system has been trying to correct is not an engineering error.

It was placed there deliberately.

Someone built an anomaly into the Ringworld and then built a system to keep trying — forever — to resolve it. As if the resolution process itself was the point.

As if something needed the Ringworld to keep running this calculation, indefinitely, producing something — data, energy, output of some kind — that no one has yet identified.

The players have stopped the loop.

But they may have also stopped something else.

## GM Notes

**Tone management:** This scenario lives or dies on atmosphere. The loop mechanic is not a puzzle to be solved mechanically — it is a feeling to be experienced. Play up the repetition. Use actual table moments — something a player said, a decision they made — and echo it back slightly wrong. This is more effective than any written text.

**Marr is your instrument:** He is the most important NPC in the scenario. He should feel real, tired, and genuine. Do not play him as mysterious. He is not withholding — he is exhausted. Everything he knows, he will tell. The tragedy is that knowing everything has not been enough.

**The ship crew:** Vorn, Talren, and Brakk are voices. Use them to create pressure and to represent the three possible attitudes to the scenario: withdrawal (Vorn), engagement (Talren), and destruction (Brakk). Let the players hear their disagreements escalating over comms. This mirrors the players' own likely disagreements.

**The system is not an enemy:** Never, at any point, should the system feel malicious. It is indifferent. It is a machine. The horror is not that it wants to hurt anyone — it is that it does not know they are there in any way that matters to it.

**The art direction:** When you display 95-ART-07 (the Core), let it sit on screen for a moment before you describe it. Silence, then the image, then: "This is what you see." Give it room.

**Player agency:** There is no correct answer at the core. All four outcomes are valid. Do not steer. Do not hint. Let them decide. The scenario is designed so that whatever they choose, it means something.
