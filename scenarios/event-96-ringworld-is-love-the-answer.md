# Event 96 – Ringworld RPG: Is Love the Answer?

---

## Overview
- **Event Number:** 96
- **System:** Ringworld RPG (1984 Edition)
- **Setting:** The Ringworld inner surface — the trading settlement of Wheel-Spoke, and the ancient structure known as the Rememberer's Vault, three miles beyond the settlement boundary
- **Tone:** Warm and wondrous at the opening, tightening to urgent and emotionally charged by Act Three. The stakes are personal before they are physical. The threat is real and the clock is running — but the hardest question is not *will we survive* but *what does doing right actually look like?*
- **Maximum Runtime:** 3 hours 30 minutes (210 minutes)
- **Players:** 6 (landing crew of the Foolish Endeavour — pre-generated characters)
- **Art Style:** Ink sketch and pencil illustration. Warm, organic linework for the Ringworld surface, settlement life, and Serenthis. Precise, angular linework for the ancient vault technology. Ghresh-Ka always rendered with heavy shadow — dominant, never cartoonish.

## Plot Summary *(GM Eyes Only)*

The landing crew sets down at Wheel-Spoke, a ring-shaped trading settlement built around the base of an ancient Ringworld spire. Their mission is a routine cultural survey. It is immediately not routine.

A young woman named Serenthis — a native Ringworldian of unusual intelligence and unusual calm — intervenes when one of the crew nearly triggers a collapse trap in the market. This is not coincidence. She was expecting them. She does not entirely understand why.

Serenthis is a Rememberer: one of a lineage whose genetic encoding carries fragments of Protector-level knowledge — not active, not transformative, but present as intuition, as deep spatial awareness, and as the ability to interface with specific ancient Ringworld systems. The Rememberers are a protected bloodline in Wheel-Spoke, known by the elders, unknown to the wider Ringworld. The arrival of off-worlders — with their technology, their alien physiologies, their casual violation of Ringworld ambient energies — has begun to *wake* the encoding in Serenthis faster than anyone anticipated.

Ghresh-Ka is a Kzin warlord who has spent eleven years on the Ringworld's inner surface, building a territory. He has learned patience the hard way. He wants Serenthis not for conquest but for *access* — he knows what a Rememberer can open, and he has spent years positioning himself to take one. He is days away with a disciplined force. He has sent his lieutenant Serin ahead to scout.

The crew is the complication Ghresh-Ka did not plan for. He will adapt.

The truth of Act Two: if Serenthis interfaces fully with the Rememberer's Vault — the ancient structure beyond the settlement — she will gain full access to a dormant Ringworld system, enough to repel Ghresh-Ka's entire force without violence. But the encoding will complete. She will begin the transition toward something like a Protector — hyper-intelligent, almost indestructible, and no longer human in the ways that matter. The crew can reach the vault. They can protect her long enough for the interface to occur. But the person who walked in will not be the person who walks out.

Meanwhile, a Ringworld weather-control event is building — not a natural storm but a triggered cascade from the shadow-square system, initiated (unknowingly) by the crew's own ship power signature. It will reach Wheel-Spoke in approximately three and a half hours. The Foolish Endeavour must break orbit and reposition before it strikes, or sustain damage the ship cannot afford.

The crew has exactly as long as the scenario lasts.

The moral dilemma: one crew member has formed a genuine, rapid, unmistakeable bond with Serenthis. At the landing craft, with the storm at their backs and Ghresh-Ka minutes behind them, she must choose — and the bonded PC must speak. There is no answer that costs nothing.

---

## Player Experience

The scenario opens warm. Wheel-Spoke is alive and strange and wonderful — the players will want to explore it. Let them, briefly. Act One's job is to make Serenthis real before the danger becomes apparent. The bond should form naturally, through play, not through instruction. The GM should identify early which player is most engaged with Serenthis and lean into that.

By mid-Act Two the players know what Serenthis is, what the vault will do to her, and what Ghresh-Ka wants. They are now managing three things simultaneously: the vault (opportunity), Ghresh-Ka's forces (threat), and Serenthis's own agency (the wildcard). She has opinions. She is not passive.

Act Three is the sprint and the gut-punch. Everything compresses. The storm and Ghresh-Ka converge. The crew reaches the landing craft. The choice lands.

The question the scenario poses — *is love the answer?* — is not answered by the GM. It is answered by the players. The four possible endings give each answer its own integrity.

## Introduction *(~15 minutes)*

### Opening
*Read aloud or paraphrase:*

> The landing craft sets down two hundred metres outside the settlement boundary, in a flat clearing that your instruments say has been used as a landing point before. How long ago is harder to determine.
>
> Wheel-Spoke sits at the base of a spire that rises — you cannot see the top of it — into the upper atmosphere. The settlement is exactly what its name implies: circular, with eight radial avenues running from a central plaza out to a ring-road market. It is old. The scrith foundation beneath the buildings is older still. What the inhabitants have built on top of it is vivid and dense and loud.
>
> Your mission is a cultural survey. Standard procedure: observe, document, do not destabilise. You have six hours before the Foolish Endeavour must reposition.
>
> That is what the mission brief says.
>
> Vorn's voice comes through the comms as you walk the perimeter toward the main entrance.
>
> *"Weather systems are doing something I don't like. Not a problem yet. Keep the mission short."*
>
> Talren's voice, overlapping: *"Gravitational resonance from the spire is affecting our instruments. I'd like samples if you can get them without incident."*
>
> Brakk's voice, last: *"If there's a problem, call early."*
>
> The market gates are open. The smell of cooking and smoke and something that might be incense reaches you fifty metres out.
>
> Inside, the crowd is enormous, diverse, and completely indifferent to your arrival.

### Player Briefing
The players are a survey team on a routine cultural mission. The settlement of Wheel-Spoke is a major trading hub — the players will not stand out initially. They are here to observe and document. The mission should take three to four hours. It will not.

**GM Aside:** At the start of the introduction, quietly identify which player character is most likely to form the bond with Serenthis. Mira Chen is the default. But watch the table — the player who is most curious, most engaged with the Ringworld as a place rather than a problem, is your bond character. Adjust accordingly. The rest of the introduction is about letting the players settle into the world before Act One begins.

## Act One — First Contact *(~60 minutes)*

> **Goal:** Establish Wheel-Spoke as a living place. Introduce Serenthis. Form the bond. Reveal Ghresh-Ka as a threat on the horizon. Act One ends when the players learn Ghresh-Ka is not "on the horizon" — he is a day away, and his lieutenant is already here.

The players arrive in the market of Wheel-Spoke. They have time to explore — encourage it. The settlement is remarkable and the players should feel that. Then: the collapse trap. Then: Serenthis. The rest of Act One is the crew learning what she is while Serin, Ghresh-Ka's lieutenant, observes from the crowd.

---

### Locations — Act One

#### The Market Ring
- **Description:** The outer ring of Wheel-Spoke is continuous market — stalls, awnings, traders of at least a dozen Ringworld hominid species. Bright fabric, bartered goods, the sounds of negotiation in four languages simultaneously. The central plaza is visible through gaps in the stalls — a large open space with the spire rising from its exact centre.
- **Atmosphere:** Warm, chaotic, alive. The players will want to engage. Let them. Give each player one market moment — an odd item, an unusual trader, a strange custom. This is the only moment of pure joy the scenario provides; use it.
- **Key Details:** At the base of one of the radial avenues, a collapsed awning has exposed the foundations of a building — old scrith with something like a maintenance hatch that has been sealed, then re-sealed, then covered with market stalls. One of these stalls, if browsed, will cause the owner to shift position — accidentally depressing a contact panel in the wall. This is the collapse trap.

`[Ringworld: Awareness — spots the instability of the stall's position before the panel is triggered. Hard difficulty. If failed, the trap triggers and the scenario proceeds.]`

#### The Collapse Trap
- **Description:** Not a weapon — a dormant Ringworld maintenance system activated by the wrong pressure sequence. The wall panel triggers a reconfiguration of the foundation scrith beneath two market stalls, causing them to drop approximately half a metre with no warning and collapsing the roof onto whoever is underneath.
- **Atmosphere:** Brief, sharp, dangerous. The crowd scatters in a circle. People help immediately — this is a community.
- **Key Details:** The player who triggers it (or is beneath it at the moment it fires) takes 1D6 damage and is pinned under debris. Before anyone from the crew reaches them — Serenthis is already there, having crossed the crowd in seconds. She places both hands on the panel in the exposed wall and the scrith lifts, releasing the trapped player. She does not appear surprised. She appears to have been expecting this moment.

`[Ringworld: First Aid — the injured player's damage is reduced to 1 if treated within 1 minute]`

#### Serenthis's Workshop
- **Description:** A narrow building on the inner ring, crammed floor to ceiling with samples, weaving tools, maps, and small Ringworld artefacts. A cot in the back. A cooking fire. The smell of something like tea. Serenthis brings the crew here to treat the injured player, and because she needs to look at them in a place where she is not watched.
- **Atmosphere:** Small, layered with personal history. The maps on the walls are significant — some are of the spire above the settlement, and they are far more detailed than any survey should be able to produce.
- **Key Details:** The artefacts include two items of note: a flat oval of unknown material that, when touched by certain people, grows warm (this is a dormant Rememberer interface token); and a set of woven bands worn on Serenthis's wrist that are actually a continuous encoded record, not decoration.

`[Ringworld: Awareness — one of the maps shows a chamber beneath the spire that does not match any public knowledge of Ringworld spire architecture]`

## Act Two — The Complication *(~90 minutes)*

> **Goal:** The crew escorts Serenthis to the vault. They learn the truth of what the interface will do to her. Ghresh-Ka's advance force arrives. The clock — the storm system and the ship's departure window — becomes critical. Act Two ends with the crew at the vault, the interface completed or not completed, and the run to the landing craft beginning under pressure.

Act Two has three moving parts running simultaneously: the vault expedition (the main thread), Ghresh-Ka's advance force pressuring the settlement (escalating threat), and the ship's comms tightening the clock. The players will feel pulled in multiple directions. This is intentional.

---

### Locations — Act Two

#### The Spire Base Passage
- **Description:** A passage at ground level on the spire's south face — covered for years by a market stall that Serenthis knows how to move. The passage descends at a gentle angle for approximately 200 metres. Scrith walls. Dim bioluminescent growth providing just enough light. The air is different — cooler, cleaner, smelling faintly of something the players cannot name.
- **Atmosphere:** Transitional. The world above recedes. Something below is becoming more present. Players with high POW may feel a subtle directional pull.
- **Key Details:** Partway down, the passage passes through an automated checkpoint — a scrith iris that has been sealed for centuries. Serenthis places her hand on the wall beside it and it opens. She is visibly shaken by the ease of it. This is the first time she has done this consciously, with knowledge of what she is doing.

`[Ringworld: Awareness — the iris shows signs of having been opened recently, perhaps three or four times in the last decade]`

#### The Rememberer's Vault
- **Description:** A circular chamber, forty metres in diameter, beneath the spire's base. Ceiling twenty metres high, domed, with passive lighting that activates fully when Serenthis enters. The walls are covered in interface panels — not the same technology as the Weather Spire in Event 95; these are older, more organic in design, as if the panels grew from the scrith rather than being embedded in it. In the chamber's centre: a raised dais with a single standing interface column, approximately two metres tall, covered in notation the players have never seen.
- **Atmosphere:** Sacred is the wrong word — it is too old for human concepts of sacred. Purposeful. As if this room was built specifically for this moment, or a moment like it, and has been waiting.
- **Key Details:** The notation on the column is in a form of Protector-script so ancient it predates any Known Space translation record. Whisper can identify it as such with a Research roll. Mira (or the bond character) may attempt translation — partial success with Computer + Linguistics; full success additionally requires Science (Physics). Full translation reveals the purpose of the vault: *it is an emergency uplift mechanism — a last resort designed so that if the Ringworld's maintenance systems failed critically, a Rememberer could accept the full encoding and become a distributed Protector-class intelligence capable of emergency repair.*

`[Ringworld: Computer + Linguistics — Hard — partial translation of the vault notation]`
`[Ringworld: Science (Physics) + Computer — Extreme — full understanding of the encoding mechanism]`

#### The Vault Entrance (Exterior)
- **Description:** At the spire base, facing the settlement. The passage entrance is here. Also here: the best defensive position in the settlement perimeter, with elevated scrith flanking and a clear view of the main road from Wheel-Spoke's eastern gate. When Ghresh-Ka's advance force arrives, they will come from the east.
- **Atmosphere:** Contested. The crew will likely establish a defensive position here — it is the obvious choice. Woven-Branch's people may appear to help or to complicate.
- **Key Details:** There are four defensive positions along the scrith flanking. The passage entrance is the choke point. If Ghresh-Ka's advance force numbers more than eight, holding this position without Ringworld assistance is approximately not survivable.

### NPCs — Act Two

#### GHRESH-KA — Kzin Warlord, Ringworld Territorial Commander

- **Physical Description:** Large even for a Kzin — nearly three metres, heavily muscled, orange-cream fur worn close at the muzzle and longer at the mane. His armour is adapted Ringworld battle-mesh over Kzin undersuit — functional, battered, *used*. One ear is half-missing. His eyes are amber and they are always calculating. He does not snarl. He does not posture. He watches.
- **Motivation:** Ghresh-Ka wants access to the Rememberer's Vault. Not Serenthis specifically — she is the *key* to the vault. What he wants is what the vault can open. He has spent eleven years on the Ringworld building a territorial base because he believes the Ringworld's systems, accessed through a Rememberer, can be directed — used as weapons, or as leverage, on a scale that rewrites Known Space politics. He is not wrong that the vault is powerful. He is catastrophically wrong about what it will do to the key.
- **What He Knows:** What a Rememberer is, broadly. That the encoding transition happens — he does not know what it produces. He believes the resulting entity will be controllable. He has planned for years on this assumption. The assumption is wrong.
- **Disposition:** Professional respect toward worthy opponents. Contempt for panic. He will offer terms once — genuine terms — before escalating. He does not enjoy unnecessary destruction. He is not cruel in the sadistic sense. He is, however, utterly committed.
- **Threat Level:** Ghresh-Ka is a sustained threat. He arrives at the settlement perimeter at the late-Act-Two mark, ahead of his full force. His advance group of six mercenaries arrives first. If the crew has not addressed Serin by this point, she opens the gate.
- **Stat Block:**
  - STR: 22 | CON: 16 | SIZ: 20 | INT: 15 | POW: 13 | DEX: 16 | APP: 08 | EDU: 12
  - HP: 18 | Move: 12 | DB: +1D6
  - Armour: Battle-mesh (3 points, covers torso)
  - Skills: Combat (claws) 85%, Combat (laser carbine) 70%, Tactics 80%, Awareness 65%, Intimidate 85%, Command 75%, Fast Talk 55%
  - Weapons: Kzin fighting claws (STR+1D6+DB), adapted laser carbine (2D6+3, 60m range), variable sword (1D6+4+DB — carried but rarely drawn; he considers it personal)
  - Special: *Warlord's Patience* — Ghresh-Ka does not charge. He manoeuvres. He will never enter a room or space he cannot exit. If cornered, he becomes more dangerous, not less. He does not rage; he focuses.

## Act Three — The Choice *(~30 minutes)*

> **Goal:** The crew reaches the landing craft with Serenthis — or without her. Ghresh-Ka intercepts them. The moral dilemma lands. The scenario ends not with a battle won but with a choice made. 30 minutes maximum. This act always happens.

---

### The Sprint

From the vault to the landing craft is three miles across open Ringworld surface. The storm — now visible as a massive geometric mass on the horizon, shadow squares shifting — is forty minutes from Wheel-Spoke. The Foolish Endeavour has less than an hour before forced repositioning. Ghresh-Ka is behind them with his full force.

Running this section: do not make it a combat encounter. Make it a film. Short, sharp exchanges. The crew moving. Ghresh-Ka closing. The storm visible in every direction.

`[Ringworld: DEX × 5 — maintain pace over rough Ringworld terrain without losing time]`
`[Ringworld: CON × 5 — sustain pace over three miles if already injured]`

---

### Location — Act Three

#### The Landing Craft Clearing

- **Description:** The same flat clearing the crew landed in two hours ago. The landing craft is there — Tomas has kept it on standby. The clearing is open. There is no cover. The storm wall is visible to the north. Ghresh-Ka's force is visible to the east, closing at pace.
- **Atmosphere:** Open. Final. The sense of the Ringworld as a vast and indifferent structure is never stronger than here — the crew are six people in an empty clearing the size of several city blocks, on a structure the size of a million Earths, with a storm on one side and a warlord on the other, and one question left.
- **Key Details:** The landing craft can lift in four minutes from standby. Ghresh-Ka's force is eight minutes away. The storm front is fifteen minutes from this position. The math is clear: there is time, but only if the crew stops doing anything else.

---

### The Interception

Ghresh-Ka does not send his force ahead. He comes himself, with two guards, and calls a halt while he is still two hundred metres out.

He speaks — calmly, loudly, in a language the crew's translators handle:

> *"I am not interested in your crew. I am not interested in your ship. I am interested in one person. Give her to me and you leave. We do not need to lose anyone today."*

This is a genuine offer. He means it. He would rather not fight a crew of off-worlders with a Known Space vessel in orbit — it creates complications he does not need. He wants Serenthis. He has waited eleven years. He can be patient for eight more minutes.

If the crew attacks, he engages with full professional competence. He is designed to be a credible threat throughout — he should feel like fighting him is survivable only with full commitment and some luck.

**If RR'raan challenges him directly:** This is a Kzin honour exchange — Ghresh-Ka will accept a one-on-one combat, pausing his force. This buys time and may end the threat. RR'raan's player should know this is their character's option.

`[Combat note: Ghresh-Ka's stats make him dangerous but not invincible. He has HP 18, Armour 3, and a +1D6 damage bonus. A focused crew can neutralise him. Victory should cost something.]`

---

### The Moral Dilemma

This is the core of the scenario. It happens at the landing craft, in the last ten minutes, with the storm building and Ghresh-Ka either neutralised, negotiating, or held at bay.

Serenthis speaks to the bond character directly. The crew can hear. She asks, quietly:

> *"What do you want me to do?"*

She is not asking for permission. She is asking because she trusts this person's answer more than she trusts her own fear. The options are hers — but this person's voice matters.

**The Four Options:**

**Option A — Come With Us**
Serenthis boards the landing craft. She leaves the Ringworld, her people, her settlement, everything she has ever known. The encoding in her is incomplete — she will live with it unresolved, perhaps forever, perhaps until it wakes again in the wrong circumstances. Ghresh-Ka is left without his key. Woven-Branch is left without her Rememberer. The bond character gets to keep her — in a form she may not remain.

*Is love taking someone from their world because you cannot bear to leave them?*

**Option B — She Stays**
The crew leaves. They cannot fight Ghresh-Ka's full force, get to the ship, and protect Serenthis simultaneously. She chooses to remain with her people. She has access to the vault — she can interface partially, on her own terms, at her own pace, with or without the full encoding. Ghresh-Ka gets his confrontation but not an easy one. Maybe she wins. Maybe she doesn't. The bond character will never know.

*Is love letting go when holding on would break what you love?*

**Option C — She Uses the Vault (if the crew reached it and the interface is possible)**
If the crew brought Serenthis to the vault and she interfaced — fully or partially — she can call what she is becoming. The storm responds. The shadow squares shift. Ghresh-Ka's force is dispersed without casualties by a weather event of impossible precision. Serenthis is standing, breathing, alive. But she is already different. In the next month, the next year, she will be something the Ringworld built her to be — not the woman who called the bond character by name in a workshop full of maps.

*Is love enough when the person you love is becoming something greater than the love you had?*

**Option D — The Crew Stays and Fights**
They miss the ship. They fight Ghresh-Ka. They may win. The ship repositions and returns in six hours — Vorn will come back. They spend six hours on the Ringworld surface in the aftermath of a storm, having just destroyed the plans of a Kzin warlord. Serenthis is alive and unchanged and the bond character chose her over the mission.

*Is love worth breaking every rule you came with?*

---

### Pacing Note — Act Three
*30 minutes maximum. At 3:10, Ghresh-Ka's terms expire — his force resumes advance. This forces a decision.*
*At 3:15, the storm front reaches the landing zone. The craft must lift now or be grounded for hours. This is the hard close.*
*The dilemma resolves in the last five minutes. Do not rush it — but do not let it drift. Ask the bond character directly: "What do you say to her?"*

## The Tangent

*After the session — a thread worth pulling.*

**The Vault Was Not Built for This**

When the crew returns to the Foolish Endeavour and Talren reviews the data collected during the expedition, he finds something that doesn't fit the vault's stated purpose.

The emergency uplift mechanism — the encoding — was designed for one specific scenario: critical Ringworld failure. The systems that would trigger it are deeply buried monitoring circuits that would only activate if the Ringworld was in danger of losing structural integrity on a catastrophic scale.

Those circuits are active.

They have been active for approximately three hundred years.

The Ringworld is not failing visibly — not yet. But something in the deep systems, below scrith, below anything the crew can access, is signalling that the Ringworld has crossed a threshold. The encoding in Serenthis's lineage was not awakening because the crew arrived. It was awakening because the *Ringworld called it.*

The crew has just resolved a personal crisis on the surface of a structure that may be dying. The Rememberer lineage was not an accident of nature or an old Protector experiment. It was maintenance protocol.

Serenthis — wherever she ended up — is a repair instruction.

The campaign question: how many Rememberer lineages exist on the Ringworld? And what happens if they are all being called simultaneously?

## GM Notes

**The bond is the scenario:** Everything else — Ghresh-Ka, the vault, the clock — is scaffolding. The scenario lives or dies on whether the connection between Serenthis and the bond character feels real before the dilemma lands. Give them time in Act One. Do not rush to the threat. Let the workshop scene breathe.

**Serenthis is a person, not a prize:** She has opinions throughout. She pushes back. She asks questions. She finds the Puppeteer alarming and the Kzin fascinating. She has a sense of humour that emerges slowly. The more real she is by Act Three, the harder the choice is.

**Ghresh-Ka is the best kind of villain:** He is not wrong about what the vault can do. He is wrong about what happens to the key. He has spent eleven years in careful preparation for a plan that has one critical flaw. Play him with dignity — a formidable, patient intelligence who has made one catastrophic miscalculation about a person's humanity.

**The moral dilemma has no right answer:** Resist any temptation to signal which outcome is "good." All four endings are honest. The title is a question, not an answer.

**The clock is your friend:** The storm and the ship's departure window are pacing tools, not punishments. Use the comms from Vorn regularly — his calm professionalism is a good contrast to the crew's increasingly complicated situation. Let the players feel the weight of the time they are spending.

**RR'raan and Ghresh-Ka:** If the Kzin player engages with this dynamic, Ghresh-Ka is an excellent foil. Ghresh-Ka represents what a Kzin becomes when patience is forced on them by circumstance. RR'raan's player may find this deeply uncomfortable or deeply resonant. Either is good.

**Whisper's revelation:** The Puppeteer player will know what the encoding means before anyone else. The moment when Whisper shares that information — if the player chooses to share it — is one of the most interesting beats in the scenario. Do not force it. But if the player is quiet about it, a gentle nudge: *"Whisper has been very still since the vault."*

**Art direction:** When you show 96-ART-07 (the clearing, Act Three), let it sit before you describe the scene. The image carries the weight of the choice before any words are spoken.
