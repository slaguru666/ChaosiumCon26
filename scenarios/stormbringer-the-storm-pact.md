## Plot Summary *(GM Only)*

Six months ago, the sorcerer Kelen — reclusive, brilliant, and dying of a slow wasting sickness — rode out to the ancient Melnibonéan ruin on the Eastern Moors known as the Spire of Kyelm. He carried a scroll of binding, a ritual dagger, and Tally's goat (borrowed without asking), which he needed as a living sacrifice to open the summoning circle.

He intended to summon and bind Vorak — a lesser Chaos demon, a being of corrosion and vitality — using Vorak's power to arrest his sickness and buy himself years he had not otherwise earned. Kelen is not a villain. He is a frightened dying man who knows too much about the world's dark corners and too little about what it costs to use them.

The ritual failed. The goat broke from its tether at the critical moment, disrupting the containment circle. Vorak was halfway through the threshold when the binding collapsed. The demon is now partially manifested: not fully in the world, not fully in the Chaos realm, anchored to the Spire in a state of building instability. Every day the binding holds less. The storms are the pressure of an unmade pact leaking into the world.

Kelen is still in the Spire. He is dying faster now — the disrupted ritual cost him — and he is trying to complete a new binding before either the demon fully manifests or he dies. He does not know which will happen first.

The players will arrive knowing only that a sorcerer disappeared, storms ruined the harvest, and someone is offering coin to fix it.

By the end of the night, they will know what Kelen wanted. They will know what it costs to unmake or complete what he started. And they will have to choose.

## Player Experience *(What the table lives)*

The players are hired in a worried village that smells of mud and storm-rot. The job seems straightforward: find a missing sorcerer, stop the storms. The NPCs are recognisable types — the worried elder, the suspicious smith, the girl demanding justice for her goat — and the world feels grounded and familiar.

Then they cross the moors, and familiar stops.

The Spire of Kyelm is older than the village, older than the roads, possibly older than the Young Kingdoms. Inside, the stonework is wrong in a way they cannot name, and the carved symbols in the binding hall are not decorative. When they find Kelen — haggard, desperate, half-mad with urgency — he tells them the truth without hesitation. He doesn't have time for deception.

The moral question arrives not as a dramatic monologue but as a practical problem: the demon is coming through regardless. The only question is whether the players shape what happens next, and at what cost to themselves.

## World Background — Mael's Ford

### What It Is
Mael's Ford is a river-crossing village on the Eastern Marches — one of the many unremarkable settlements that make up the Young Kingdoms' agricultural backbone. Perhaps three hundred souls. A mill, a smithy, a tavern, a hall where the elder holds court. The river runs high in storm season; the ford is sometimes impassable for weeks. The surrounding land is farmland and moor in equal measure.

The village does not think of itself as being in the shadow of the Melnibonéan empire. The Spire of Kyelm has been on the eastern moor for as long as anyone can remember. Children are told not to go near it. They don't. This has kept the Spire largely undisturbed for four centuries.

### What the Storm Has Done
Three weeks of unnatural storms have wrecked the harvest. Half the root crops are rotting in waterlogged fields. The mill is flooded; the wheel hasn't turned in ten days. Two cattle were struck by lightning. One family's roof collapsed. The village is not in immediate danger of starvation, but it is in the slow anxiety of a community watching its margin disappear.

### Law and Chaos in the Eastern Marches
The villagers do not think in terms of Law and Chaos. They think in terms of bad weather, a missing sorcerer, and whether this winter will be hard. The players may be more theologically aware — depending on their backgrounds — but the villagers will look at them blankly if they start talking about the Cosmic Balance.

### Tone at the Table
Mael's Ford feels ordinary. Keep it ordinary as long as possible. The shift happens when the players leave the village — the moors are where the world becomes strange.

## Running the Young Kingdoms — GM World Texture Guide
*Reference for language and atmosphere during play. Do not read aloud — use as a source of sensory detail.*

### What Things Cost
The Young Kingdoms use Melnibonéan gold, silver, and copper. For practical purposes: a meal costs a few coppers, a night in the village tavern is a silver, a skilled hireling earns a silver a day, and the Elder's reward of ten gold pieces per person is three months' wages for a village craftsman. The players are not rich. Ten gold apiece matters.

### The Moors at Night
The Eastern Moors are not supernatural — they are simply large, dark, and far from the nearest light. The grass is long and resistant underfoot. There is no path to the Spire from Mael's Ford — Joran knows a route by landmarks, and so does Tally. The Spire is visible from the moors on a clear night: a broken finger of black stone against the sky. On a storm night, it is barely visible at all.

The smell on the moors when Vorak's disrupted binding is leaking: ozone and something older — not sulphur, but the mineral cold of very deep stone.

### The Spire of Kyelm — Exterior
Half-collapsed. Ancient Melnibonéan construction: bone-white stone with iridescent green mineral veins, the result of building materials quarried in an age when the Dragon Isle was still inhabited. The tower is three floors, the fourth reduced to rubble. Arrow-slit windows. A gate arch — the door is long gone. Carved around the arch: Melnibonéan binding glyphs, worn but legible to anyone with the relevant Lore skill.

At night, a faint blue-white luminescence leaks from the windows. Not firelight. Not moonlight. Vorak's influence bleeding through the stone.

### The Spire — Interior Atmosphere
Each floor is colder than the last until the Summoning Spire at the top, which is warm and wrong. The air inside smells of stone and old ritual residue — incense and blood, both old. Kelen's recent presence has added tallow candles, the chemical smell of reagents, and the sharp sour smell of a sick man who has been living alone for weeks.

Sound carries strangely inside the Spire. Voices echo from the wrong direction. Players will notice this but cannot explain it. It is not a trap or a puzzle. It is just old stone shaped by old purposes.

### Useful Phrases
- *"The Balance turns"* — a Chaos cultist's greeting; ominous when heard in the wrong context
- *"Taken by the pact"* — local phrase for what happened to Kelen, now used by the villagers
- *"Law's mercy"* — a genuine prayer; Lian uses it unself-consciously
- *"Naming-cost"* — what a demon demands in exchange for service; the demon's price

## Introduction *(~15 minutes)*

### Opening — Read Aloud or Paraphrase

> The storms have been running three weeks without a break. Today there is a gap — the sky is the colour of old iron, but it is not raining, and the folk of Mael's Ford have taken that as the closest thing to a good omen they have seen in a month.
>
> Elder Thara meets you in the village hall — a low-ceilinged room that smells of wet wood and tallow. She is a compact woman with silver braids and a ceremonial cloak that has seen better times. She does not sit. She plants both hands on the table and looks at each of you in turn.
>
> "The sorcerer Kelen disappeared six weeks ago. He lived on the edge of the village, kept to himself, caused no trouble. Three days after he left, the storms started. We don't know what he did or where he went. What we know is that our harvest is ruined, our mill is stopped, and last night something killed two of Joram's cattle — and it wasn't lightning."
>
> She slides a purse across the table. "Ten gold pieces each. Find Kelen, stop the storms, tell us what happened. That's what we're paying for."

### Player Briefing
Elder Thara provides the job verbally and with Handout SP-H00:

- Kelen, local sorcerer, disappeared approximately six weeks ago
- Storms began three days after his disappearance
- She believes the storms are connected to whatever Kelen was doing
- Payment: 10 gold per person on resolution
- She does not know what Kelen was doing and is honest about that

**What Thara will NOT say:** That she knew Kelen was ill. That she sent someone to check on him two weeks ago and they came back "confused" and could not say why they had turned back before the Spire. That she is more frightened than she is letting on.

`[Stormbringer: Insight 40% — Thara knows more than she is saying. The gap is not deception; it is a village elder protecting her authority while privately terrified.]`

### Character Introductions
After the briefing, give players two minutes each: who they are, why they took the job, what they notice about Mael's Ford. Sarath will be quieter than usual during the briefing. Cray will already be looking for exits. Lian will ask one too many earnest questions.

## ACT ONE — Mael's Ford *(~30 minutes)*
> **Goal:** Ground the players in the village world. Deliver the essential clues. Introduce the key NPCs with enough texture that they become people rather than information dispensers. End with the wolf attack and the road to the moors.

### Location: The Village Square

**Description:** The square is where the ford road meets the mill road. A well in the centre, currently surrounded by anxious people checking the water level. The smell of wet thatch and storm-sour mud. A child's wooden horse lies in a puddle near the well.

**Atmosphere:** Functional anxiety. The village is working, but everything has that slight extra urgency of people who are watching the weather more than they used to.

**Key Details:**
- Elder Thara will direct them to Joran (blacksmith) for information about Kelen's movements and to Mira (herbalist) for anything about the moors
- `[Stormbringer: Spot 30% — the cattle pen near the east gate is patched with fresh timber. Something broke through it recently. Not from the outside.]`
- Tally will find the players. She cannot be avoided. She is standing on the well and will not get down until someone agrees to hear about her goat.

---

### Location: The Blacksmith's Forge

**Description:** An open-sided shed at the south edge of the square. Glowing embers, tools in careful order, an anvil polished by decades of use. Joran works even when the village is in distress — possibly especially when the village is in distress.

**Atmosphere:** Gritty, warm, industrially ordinary. The cleanest-smelling place in Mael's Ford right now.

**Joran when approached:** Puts down the work. Looks at the players like he is calculating their worth before speaking.

**What Joran will say:**
- He last saw Kelen loading supplies onto a cart six weeks ago. "More rope than you need for anything good, and a caged animal. Thara's girl's goat, though he didn't ask."
- He knows the direction Kelen went: northeast, toward the moors. Toward the old ruin.
- He has a personal grudge: Kelen borrowed his best chisel two years ago and never returned it. He suspects it is in the Spire. He is half-serious.
- "There's a shape to those storms. They don't move like weather. They circle."

`[Stormbringer: Insight 40% — Joran believes Kelen is already dead. He is not going to say this in front of Thara or anyone who might repeat it. He thinks the village is paying for a corpse retrieval and doesn't know it.]`

**[ESSENTIAL CLUE — #1]** Kelen went northeast. The animal was a goat. The cart was loaded for ritual work.

---

### Location: Mira's Herb Garden (behind the tavern)

**Description:** A sheltered garden that is doing better than most things in the village right now because Mira covers it when storms approach. Dried herbs hanging in the lean-to. The smell of lavender and something more pungent.

**Atmosphere:** Unexpectedly calm. A corner of the village that feels slightly outside the anxiety.

**Mira — young, watchful, precise — will say:**
- Three nights before the storms, she saw lights on the moors. Not firelight — blue-white, flickering like lightning but from the ground up, not the sky down.
- The lights came from the direction of "the old tower that nobody names."
- She went to tell Elder Thara. Thara told her she had seen sheet lightning and sent her home.
- Mira does not think she saw sheet lightning.

`[Stormbringer: Lore (Young Kingdoms) 30% — the colour and direction of the lights match historical descriptions of a summoning circle at the point of opening. This is documented. The document is in a library in Karlaak. Ysolde knows this without the roll.]`

**[ESSENTIAL CLUE — #2]** The lights were from the Spire, not the sky. The ritual started three nights before the storms.

---

### Location: Old Harrek's Corner (the tavern's end booth)

**Description:** Old Harrek has occupied the same corner booth for twelve years. He smells of pipe smoke and old campaigns. His drinking cup is older than most people in the village.

**Atmosphere:** The specific quality of a man who has seen the world and decided it is best observed from a seated position.

**Old Harrek — if bought a drink — will say:**
- There is a Chaos cult operating in the Eastern Marches. He has seen their trail-marks on trees twice in the past month. They do not have a fixed camp.
- They worship "something old that got let out somewhere." His words.
- He will not go near the Spire. He will not say why. It's not cowardice; it's the considered decision of a man who has calculated his remaining years and their value.

`[Stormbringer: Fast Talk or Persuade 40% — Harrek will add that one of the cult members is very young, very scared, and was asking questions about Kelen's ruin three weeks ago. "Asking questions like someone who knows the answer and doesn't want to be right."]`

**[OPTIONAL CLUE]** A Chaos cult has taken interest in Kelen's work. They have not yet reached the Spire. If the players move slowly, they might.

---

### NPC: Tally — The Village's Loudest Resident

Tally attaches herself to the party in the village square. She is sixteen, relentless, equipped with a wooden flute she plays badly, and entirely convinced that whatever is happening started with her goat.

She is correct. She doesn't know she is correct. This is worse.

**What Tally will say (in order of urgency):**
- "His name was Bran. He was not a ritual sacrifice. He was a good goat and he knew tricks."
- "Kelen took him without asking. I want Bran back or I want paying. And I want to come."
- She knows a shortcut to the Spire via a shepherd's path that cuts 20 minutes off the moor crossing. She will only share it if allowed to come or if a player makes a Persuade roll at 50%.
- "My brother went to look for Kelen three months ago. He didn't come back properly. He was here for two days and then he just... left again. Toward the moors."

`[Stormbringer: Insight 50% — Tally is describing a pattern of voluntary return — something drawing people back to the Spire against their reason. She does not know the name for this. Cray knows who Sevel is.]`

**[ESSENTIAL CLUE — #3]** Sevel (Cray's contact) was drawn back to the Spire voluntarily. The demon has been leaking into the surrounding area for longer than the storms.

### Encounter: The Three Chaos-Touched Wolves

As the players set out across the moors — with or without Tally — the encounter happens at the treeline where the village ends and the moor begins. The storms have not broken; the sky is the heavy grey of held breath.

The wolves emerge from the long grass at dusk. Three of them, and wrong: matted fur crossed with veins of lightning-blue — not glowing exactly, but with a quality like foxfire caught in the coat. Their eyes are a flat silver. They move in coordination that wolves do not naturally manage.

These are not Chaos demons. They are animals that have been too close to Vorak's leaked influence for too long and have been changed by it. They are afraid and in pain. They will not stop.

**Per Wolf:**
STR: 14 | CON: 13 | SIZ: 11 | INT: 6 | POW: 12 | DEX: 15
HP: 12 | DB: +1D4 | Move: 13
**Attack:** Bite 50%, 1D8+DB
**Dodge:** 35%
**Armour:** Thick matted fur (2 points)
**Special:** Any character bitten must roll POW×5 or feel a moment of disorientation at the start of their next action — describe it as a flash-image of the Spire seen from a great height, from Vorak's perspective. Mechanically: -10% to one skill roll of the GM's choice on the following round. Do this once per character, maximum.
**Morale:** If two wolves are killed, the third breaks and runs toward the Spire — not away from it. This is not courage. This is the pull.

`[Stormbringer: Any player who succeeds on a Spot roll during the fight: the fleeing wolf is not running away. It is going toward something.]`

### Pacing Note — Act One
> If running more than 5 minutes over at the 0:45 mark, compress Harrek entirely and move Tally's information about Sevel to a line she delivers as they leave the village. Essential clues to land before Act Two: Kelen's direction (Joran), the lights (Mira), Sevel's return (Tally). The wolf fight should run no more than 10 minutes.

## ACT TWO — The Moors and the Spire of Kyelm *(~90 minutes)*
> **Goal:** Escalate from grounded to uncanny to genuinely strange. Deliver the history of Kelen's ritual. Confront the players with Vorak's reality. Force the encounter with Kelen and the decision to ascend.

### Location: The Moor Crossing

**Description:** The walk to the Spire takes 40 minutes on the shepherd's path (20 minutes longer without it). The moors at night are genuinely dark — the cloud cover kills any moonlight — and the only landmark is the Spire itself, visible when lightning flashes.

**Atmosphere:** The ozone smell of the disrupted binding, stronger with every half-mile. The ground becomes wetter and stranger underfoot — the grass grows in patterns that are not quite random, particularly near the old drainage channels. No animals. No sounds except the players' movement and the distant grumble of storm.

`[Stormbringer: Navigate or Spot 35% — the grass is growing in radial patterns centred on the Spire. Whatever Vorak is leaking, it is affecting the ecology in a slow, widening ring.]`

**[OPTIONAL CLUE]** The radius of the affected grass is approximately one mile. The ecology has been disturbed for six weeks.

---

### Location: The Gate Arch

**Description:** The Spire's entrance is an archway in the ruined outer wall. The door is long gone. The arch is carved with Melnibonéan binding glyphs — repeated containment symbols, worn but legible.

**Atmosphere:** The blue-white light from inside is visible now: not a flicker, more a pulse, regular as breathing.

`[Stormbringer: Lore (Melnibonéan) 40% — the glyphs are containment wards, originally set to prevent demons from exiting the Spire unbidden. Most have worn away. The few that remain would have slowed Vorak's manifestation. Without them it would already be in the world.]`

`[Stormbringer: Insight 30% — the ward glyphs are being worn away from the inside. Whatever is in there has been pushing against them for weeks.]`

Inside the arch, scratched at knee height in fresh work (days old, not years): a single Chaos cult symbol. Someone else has been here recently.

**[ESSENTIAL CLUE — #4]** The wards have been the only thing slowing Vorak's full manifestation. They are nearly gone.

---

### Location: The Binding Hall (Ground Floor)

**Description:** A large circular chamber taking up the entire ground floor of the Spire. Ancient stone, perfectly fitted — Melnibonéan construction at its most clinical. The floor is cleared stone except for the binding circle: a geometric diagram of unusual complexity, approximately four metres across, burned into the floor with a heat that has blackened the stone but not cracked it.

The circle is incomplete. At one point — the eastern arc, near a deep gouge in the floor — the line breaks. Dried blood and something that was, once, a goat.

**Atmosphere:** The room smells of old ritual, fresh failure, and something underneath both: a mineral cold that rises from the circle itself. Vorak's presence is strongest here. The blue-white light has no visible source.

`[Stormbringer: Lore (Melnibonéan) or Lore (Chaos) 40% — the circle is a binding diagram of genuine sophistication. Kelen knew what he was doing. The interruption was not incompetence; it was circumstance. Ysolde can identify this without a roll.]`

**Handout SP-H01** — Kelen's working notes fragment: found tucked under a loose stone near the circle. Torn, partial, ink-blotted. Formulae and marginalia. On the last legible fragment: *"Vorak will not take payment in ordinary coin. It requires vitality — a life's surplus. What the goat has, I do not. The circle must hold until the exchange is complete. If it does not hold—"* The sentence ends. The page is torn here.

**Handout SP-H02** — The binding circle diagram: a careful sketch of the circle as it exists now. The break in the eastern arc is marked. Players who study it can identify where the original containment failed.

`[Stormbringer: Insight 50% — Vorak is not attacking the ward glyphs from malice. It is an anchored entity in an incomplete binding — it has nowhere to go, cannot fully manifest, cannot return to Chaos. It is, in a manner of speaking, trapped as much as the land is. This does not make it safe. It makes it desperate.]`

**[ESSENTIAL CLUE — #5]** The binding failed at the eastern arc. Vorak has been half-manifested ever since. The storms are the pressure of an unmade pact.

### Encounter: The Bound Fragment (Binding Hall)

Vorak cannot move freely. But in the six weeks of instability, the demon's presence has condensed into a secondary manifestation in the Binding Hall — a knot of Chaos energy given crude form, barely sentient, following the simplest imperative: protect the circle.

This is not Vorak. It is a fragment of Vorak's influence given temporary shape.

**The Bound Fragment:**
STR: 12 | CON: 14 | SIZ: 10 | INT: 4 | POW: 14 | DEX: 13
HP: 10 | DB: None | Move: 10
**Attack:** Chaos lash (a tendril of blue-white energy) 45%, 1D6 — ignores armour
**Dodge:** 30%
**Special:** Any character struck must roll POW vs the Fragment's POW 14 or suffer a temporary Chaos manifestation (cosmetic only, lasts 1D4 hours — a translucent quality to the skin, faint blue veins visible at the temples, eyes briefly silver). Mechanically harmless. Socially alarming.
**Nature:** Not destroyed by death — it disperses. It takes 1D4 rounds to reconstitute. It will stop attacking if the players move to the staircase and ascend. It is guarding the circle, not pursuing.

`[Stormbringer: Sarath recognises this immediately: "Don't kill it. It's not the demon — it's a piece of the binding that got loose. If you destroy it properly you might break the last wards." He is correct.]`

### Location: The Warden's Chamber (Second Floor)

**Description:** A smaller room, once used by the Melnibonéan staff who managed the Spire. Kelen has been living here: straw bedroll, food supplies (mostly eaten), multiple candles (mostly burned), and a portable writing desk covered in papers.

**Atmosphere:** The specific quality of a man working against time in a very small space. Urgency and organisation coexisting. Kelen is not chaotic in his personality — he is precise, methodical, and dying. The papers show this.

**Handout SP-H03** — Kelen's confession letter: written carefully, unlike the working notes. Clearly intended to be found:

> *I am Kelen. I was a sorcerer of modest gifts and immodest ambition. I have the wasting sickness — my physician gave me eight months two years ago, and she was generous.*
>
> *Vorak is a demon of vitality and corrosion — what is alive, it sustains; what is failing, it reclaims. I summoned it to offer a bargain: I would give it anchor in the world, a vessel, a purpose — in exchange for the years I am owed.*
>
> *The circle broke. The goat is to blame — it is also my fault for using a borrowed animal that had not been ritually prepared. The binding is incomplete. Vorak cannot fully manifest; I cannot complete the exchange. The storms are the pressure of this.*
>
> *I am in the Summoning Spire, attempting a second binding. I do not have much time. I do not ask forgiveness. I ask whoever finds this to understand that there are no good options here — only less-bad ones.*
>
> *The demon is not evil. It is what it is. I am not evil. I am dying. We were both making a practical arrangement. The world is worse at arrangements than it pretends.*
>
> *— K.*

`[Stormbringer: Research or Lore (Chaos) 40% — Vorak is a genuine lesser Chaos demon, specifically a Vitalic Demon (Vorak type): it sustains living things by draining the Chaos entropy that causes decay. Binding it properly would genuinely arrest Kelen's sickness. The exchange Kelen proposed is theoretically valid — not corrupt, not predatory. Just very high risk.]`

**[ESSENTIAL CLUE — #6]** Kelen's motivation is sympathetic. The demon is not malicious. The binding is still possible. There are three ways forward.

### The Horror Escalation Points in Act Two
*Distribute across the act. Spacing is everything.*

1. **The pulse** *(Gate Arch)*: The blue-white light from inside the Spire pulses in time with something the players cannot hear. Tell one player privately: "Your character notices their own heartbeat has aligned with the pulse. It has been doing this for several minutes without your awareness."

2. **Sevel's mark** *(Binding Hall, if Cray is present)*: On the floor near the gouge in the circle, Cray finds a piece of fabric — distinctive pattern. It is from Sevel's cloak. He was here recently. He left it, or it was left of him. `[Cray: tell the player privately. Give them a moment before continuing.]`

3. **The Fragment's manifestation** *(as described above)*: Keep it brief, keep it clinical. The cosmetic Chaos effect is more unsettling than the fight.

4. **Kelen's letter** *(Warden's Chamber)*: Read it aloud or let a player read it. Pause afterward. Let the table process that this is a sympathetic man, a feasible plan, and a catastrophe that was a goat's fault.

### Finding Kelen: The Summoning Spire (Third Floor, Interior Stairs)

The stairs are passable but tight. As the players approach the third floor, the temperature rises — not gradually, but in a clear threshold as they cross the level boundary. The blue-white light is intense here. The air tastes of ozone and something that is almost metallic.

**Before they enter:** A voice. Kelen — dry, precise, the voice of a man who has been talking to himself for weeks. "If you're from Mael's Ford, you're too late to stop me and I don't expect you to help. If you're from the cult, you're mistaken about what this is. Which are you?"

The correct answer is neither. Or both. He will open the door regardless — he needs witnesses, or perhaps just another human voice.

**Kelen when the door opens:** Male, early sixties. Gaunt in the specific way of long illness — the bones showing through the skin rather than the flesh disappearing. Worn robes with Chaos sigils he has been adding over the six weeks of the failed binding. White hair, mostly wild. Hands shaking, but controlled; the shaking is in the tendons, not the decisions.

He does not apologise. He explains.

**What Kelen tells them:**
- The first binding failed because of the goat. He is still angry about the goat, even now.
- Vorak is three-quarters manifested. The final quarter will come through in hours, not days, whether he completes the binding or not. If it comes through unbound: the storms become permanent, and Vorak — confused, anchored to nothing, acting on pure Chaos imperative — will begin consuming the vitality of the surrounding land and population. "Not quickly. Not dramatically. Just... steadily."
- A completed binding requires either a willing soul to anchor it (Kelen himself will do this — it kills him faster but cleanly) or an external sacrifice of significant Power.
- "There is a third option. You could bind it to an object. It won't be happy about that. But it will be contained."
- He knows about the cult. "They want to let it through unbound. They think an unbound Chaos demon will elevate them. They are wrong in the way idealists are always wrong."

`[Stormbringer: Insight 50% — Kelen is not asking to be saved. He has made his peace with this. He is asking the players to choose what happens next, because he needs at least one set of capable hands in the room when Vorak finishes coming through.]`

## ACT THREE — The Summoning Spire *(~30 minutes)*
> **Goal:** The choice. Whatever the players decide, they must decide it now, with the demon halfway through the threshold and a dying man watching. No clean answers. The end pivots on what they are willing to lose or embrace.

### The Arrival of Vorak

As the players speak with Kelen, the light in the room intensifies. The Spire shudders — not structurally, but in the way a room changes when something large enters it. Vorak's final passage has begun.

The demon does not appear dramatically. It condenses: a presence in the room that the senses cannot agree on. It is warm where the room should be cold. It is large where there should be space. It has a quality of wrongness that is not malice but sheer ontological difference — it is made of different laws than the players are, and it has just arrived in theirs.

`[Stormbringer: Sanity — does not exist in Stormbringer 1e as a mechanic. Substitute: POW resistance vs Vorak's POW 18. Failure means one round of paralysis and an immediate permanent -1 POW from proximity to uncontrolled Chaos. Offer the table a description first: this is not human fear. This is the kind of awe that precedes fear. The kind that makes you forget you have hands.]`

Vorak is not hostile. It is also not patient. The half-binding is resolved in the next few minutes, or it resolves itself.

**Vorak:**
STR: 22 | CON: 20 | SIZ: 18 | INT: 14 | POW: 18 | DEX: 10
HP: 19 | PP: 18 | Move: varies
**Nature:** Cannot be conventionally killed. Killing its current form disperses it back to Chaos — which does not solve the ward collapse or the storm. Weapons deal half damage. Chaos-attuned weapons (Sarath's, if he has used it) deal full damage.
**Attacks:** Not attacking — yet. If the players attack Vorak without attempting a binding: it defends (Chaos-lash at 60%, 1D10+special, POW vs POW 18 or -2 permanent POW). It will destroy them if pressed. Do not let it come to this — make the options visible first.

---

### The Moral Choice
*Present all three with equal weight. Do not telegraph a preference. Step back and let the table decide.*

---

**Option 1 — Kelen's Own Binding (The Sacrifice)**

Kelen completes the original intent: binding Vorak to himself as vessel and anchor. The demon's vitality arrests Kelen's sickness. Kelen lives — for a year, perhaps two, perhaps less — bound to Vorak, part-demon, consuming entropy to sustain himself. When Kelen dies, Vorak returns to Chaos. The binding holds until then.

*What it requires:* Kelen performing the binding ritual (takes 5 minutes, during which the party must hold the space — no other threats, no interruptions). If the Chaos cult arrives during this time, the party has a fight.

*What it costs:* Kelen gets to live, but not as himself. He will change. He knows this. "I will be a thing of Chaos within two months. But the storms will stop. The land will recover. And I will make my peace with what I become, which is more than most people manage."

*What it leaves:* A Chaos-bound sorcerer loose in the Young Kingdoms. The players let this happen. The cult will be interested in him.

---

**Option 2 — Bind Vorak to an Object (The Tainted Bargain)**

The demon is bound into a weapon or vessel the party provides. The storms stop. The land recovers. Vorak is constrained.

*What it requires:* The binding requires 1D4 permanent POW from whoever performs it (Ysolde, Sarath, or Lian — each has reason to hesitate). Sarath has the binding scroll; using it costs him the scroll and 1D4 POW. Ysolde can perform it from her research knowledge at the same cost. Lian can channel Law's power — which costs 1D6 permanent POW and requires Lian to bind a Chaos demon, which the Church of Law will have opinions about.

*What it costs:* The bound object exists. It is powerful, Chaos-tainted, and Vorak — even bound — will push against whoever carries it. The carrier feels it: small impulses, strange dreams, an occasional clarity that comes from somewhere else. Vorak is not malicious. But it is not Law. The carrier has to decide, every day, how much they trust the clarity.

*What it leaves:* The demon is in the world, contained but present. Someone is carrying it. That someone will have to keep carrying it.

---

**Option 3 — Let Vorak Through Unbound (The Abdication)**

The players do nothing, or cannot agree in time. Vorak completes the passage uncontained. The storms stop — Vorak does not need the pressure anymore. But the vitality drain on the surrounding land begins. Slowly. The crops next year will be poor. The year after, the livestock will begin to sicken. In five years, Mael's Ford will be a village of sick people wondering what went wrong. In ten, it will be empty.

*What it costs:* Nothing tonight. Everything over the next decade.

`[Stormbringer: If the players attempt to combine Option 1 and 2 — have Kelen bind himself while the party also attempts an object binding — this is mechanically possible. Two binding attempts require two performers. Ask both players to roll simultaneously: Summon/Bind vs Vorak's POW 18. If both succeed: double binding, Vorak is constrained twice. Kelen's progression slows. This is the best outcome, and it costs the most POW. Let the table find this if they look.]`

### Pacing Note — Act Three
> At the 2:45 mark, if unresolved: Vorak completes the passage. The question is now Option 2 or Option 3 — Option 1 takes time Kelen no longer has. Force the moment by having the light in the room become too bright to look at directly. The question is simple: does someone make the binding or not?

## Epilogue / Conclusion *(~15 minutes)*

**If Kelen completed the self-binding (Option 1):**
The storms stop that night. The ward glyphs, no longer under pressure, hold for whatever remains of Kelen's life. Mael's Ford gets its harvest in late, damaged but not ruined. Kelen leaves the Spire and disappears into the Young Kingdoms — going where, he does not say. Three months later, a report comes from a village two days east: a gaunt old man has been seen on the moors, following the deer herds. He is keeping the deer alive through a hard winter. The deer do not run from him.

**If the party completed an object binding (Option 2):**
The storms stop within the hour. Kelen, no longer useful to the binding, tells the party where the ritual dagger is hidden (it is worth money) and sits down. He is going to die here, but slowly now, and without urgency. The bound object goes with whoever is carrying it. The first dream comes three days later. It is not a nightmare. It is a very clear image of a deer on a moor, surviving a winter it should not survive. The carrier wakes up with the clear, sourceless knowledge that forty-seven deer are alive that were not going to be. This knowledge is accurate. It does not stop.

**If nothing was done (Option 3):**
The storms stop. Mael's Ford celebrates for a day. Elder Thara pays the party without asking too many questions. In three years, a healer from Karlaak visits the village on an unrelated matter and notes in her journal that the local population shows an unusual rate of vitality depletion — "as if something in the regional environment is drawing on the residents' constitutions. I recommend they relocate, though I cannot say why I am certain of this."

**If Reth — Kelen — survives any ending:**
Kelen is not a villain who needs to be dealt with. He is a dying man who made a desperate choice. What happens to him after is a function of what the party chose, and how they treated him during. If they were hostile, he disappears without a word. If they were not — if they gave him the basic dignity of explanation and choice — he will, eventually, send a letter. It will be brief, precise, and unexpectedly kind. It will not say what he is now.

## GM Notes — Running The Storm Pact

### The Tone Problem
This scenario lives or dies on Kelen being sympathetic. He is not a villain who made a deal for power. He is a frightened dying man who tried to buy time and made a technical error. If the players find him before Act Three and want to simply stop his binding, let them try — and let him explain, quietly and without drama, that stopping the binding at this stage does not help. The binding is happening. The question is only how it ends.

Do not play Kelen as desperate in a theatrical way. He is past desperation. He is in the calm on the other side of it. This is more unsettling, and more human.

### Vorak — Do Not Make It a Monster
Vorak is not hostile. It is enormous, alien, and disturbing to human senses, but it is not attacking. It arrived to make a deal. The deal went wrong. It is waiting, in the way that things with no concept of human time wait — without impatience, which is its own kind of horror.

The players will want to fight it. Let them know, through Kelen or through Sarath, that fighting it does not resolve the situation. If they still want to fight it: that is their choice. Let them. Then describe what an uncontrolled Chaos demon does to a region when the last ward glyphs collapse, and let them sit with that.

### The Moral Choice — Do Not Help Them
As with any good scenario of this kind: present all three options with equal weight and step back. Players will look for a clean answer. There is none. Option 1 puts a Chaos-bound sorcerer in the world. Option 2 puts Vorak in someone's hands. Option 3 puts Vorak's hunger in the land. All three are genuinely bad. All three have something to recommend them.

The players who engage with this in good faith and make a hard choice will have a better session than the players who try to find the trick. Reward good faith. The world of the Young Kingdoms is not a puzzle to be solved — it is a situation to be survived.

### Tally and the Spire
Tally will try to enter the Spire. She has a right to know what happened to Sevel. The players must decide whether she comes in. If she does: she should be protected, not used; she is sixteen and entirely unprepared for Vorak. If she doesn't: she waits outside and is attacked by a Chaos-touched wolf at the worst narrative moment (two hours in, when the party is deep in the ruin). Neither option is free.

### The Chaos Cult
The cult does not need to appear in the scenario unless the game is running fast and the table wants more combat. If they appear: they arrive at the Spire during Act Three, try to prevent any binding, and make the timing problem significantly worse. Dael — the former Law devotee who now leads them — is a good mirror for Lian if both are at the table. Do not use this unless you have time.

### Pacing Across Four to Six Players
The scenario is designed around three essential skill-based information moments (Joran, Mira, Kelen's letter) and one major roleplay moment (Kelen in person). Make sure each player has a task at each location — Ysolde is the expert at the ruin, Sarath knows Vorak's type, Cray finds Sevel's fragment, Lian holds the moral weight of Act Three. Distribute actively. The quiet players will surprise you if given the moment.
