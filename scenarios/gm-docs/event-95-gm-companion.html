The Truth

GM Eyes Only — Full Plot

Three hundred years ago, the Ringworld's distributed management system detected a mass imbalance in this region — a structural inconsistency that should not exist given the engineering tolerances of the structure. Left uncorrected, it compounds. The system initiated a containment protocol: isolate the region, simulate a gravitational collapse to model the inconsistency, recalculate structural load, reset conditions, repeat. An iterative solution engine — mechanical, patient, relentless.

The problem: conscious observers. Every time beings enter the region, they introduce chaotic variables into the calculation. So the system resets. The region is caught in a loop — not of time exactly, but of conditions. Physical matter resets. Energy states reset. Memory, however, does not fully reset for beings with sufficient cognitive complexity.

Dr. Silev Marr has been caught in the loop for what feels to him like years. He has lost count of iterations. He is not mad — he is exhausted. He knows more about this region than anyone alive, because he has died here hundreds of times and woken up at the start of each cycle.

The deeper problem: the system is failing. Its iterative process has exceeded its own predictive limits. Each reset leaves a small residue of mass. The simulated singularity is accumulating real mass. What began as a calculation is becoming a physical event. If nothing interrupts it, the singularity will become self-sustaining within hours. The Foolish Endeavour will be pulled in.

The system is not malicious. It does not want to destroy anything. But it will accept input — if the players can communicate in terms it recognises: mathematical data, physical action on the mass source, or complete withdrawal of conscious variables from the region.

GM Tone Notes

The Loop at the Table

Use actual table moments. When the loop manifests, repeat something a player actually said — slightly wrong. "You said that before" is only effective if it really happened. Keep a note of two or three small moments to echo back.

Marr Is Your Instrument

He is not mysterious. He is not withholding. He is exhausted. Play him as a scientist who has been right every time and it has never been enough. Everything he knows, he will tell. The tragedy is that knowing hasn't helped.

The System Is Not an Enemy

Never, at any point, should the system feel malicious. It is indifferent the way physics is indifferent. The horror is that it does not know the players are there in any way that matters to it.

Ship Crew as Chorus

Vorn = withdrawal. Talren = engagement. Brakk = destruction. These are the three possible responses to the scenario made vocal over comms. Let their disagreements escalate as the situation worsens. They are mirrors for the table.

Introduction

~15 min
The Foolish Endeavour dropped from hyperdrive six hours ago and has been holding position since.

The Ringworld fills the forward viewport — not as a planet but as a wall, a horizon that curves upward in both directions and never ends. You can make out the shadow squares drifting in their slow procession and the faint smear of weather systems moving across a surface the size of a million Earths.

Chief Navigator Talren has not left the instruments since arrival. He keeps rechecking the same figures. Captain Vorn has twice asked him if there is an error. Both times, Talren has said the same thing:

"There is no error. That is the problem."

The anomaly is there on every screen. A circular region approximately forty kilometres in diameter on the Ringworld's inner surface. Weather systems stop at its edge as if hitting a wall. Gravitational readings within the boundary are absent. A perfect, engineered nothing. And at its centre: a gravitational signature that your instruments say should not be physically possible at this scale.

Brakk has already checked the weapons systems twice.
Before Descent — Establish (1) Ship maintains orbit, comms open at all times. (2) Vorn's standing order: observe, do not interfere with Ringworld systems. (3) Talren aside: mass signature is growing, slowly but measurably. (4) Brakk's position: any unknown mechanism should be assumed hostile.

Act One — The Surface

~60 min · Goal: establish the loop as mystery

The players explore, find evidence of others, meet Marr, and experience their first loop event. They should leave Act One unsettled but not yet understanding what they face.

Locations

The Boundary

A perfectly circular line where the Ringworld's surface ecology simply stops. Nothing violent — the wrongness is in the physics. Air pressure slightly different. Sound echoes arrive before they should. Instruments flicker within 10m of the line, then stabilise. Biological functions feel subtly out of sync for ~30 seconds after crossing.

[Ringworld: Awareness — notices the sound anomaly]
 
[Ringworld: Science (Physics) — understands active field maintenance]
The Quiet Zone — Open Ground

Absolute silence. No plants, insects, wind. The sky is normal — arc of surface visible, shadow squares drifting — but weather stops at the boundary edge above as surely as it does on the ground. Clouds pile against invisible walls. Equipment scattered: survey pack, comm unit, a single boot. Cold. Undamaged. Footprints in impossible configurations — overlapping, some ending mid-step.

[Ringworld: Awareness — impossible track configurations, different depths]
 
[Ringworld: Science (Survey) — survey pack is RSC issue, model discontinued 40 years ago]
Marr's Camp

Rough shelter from salvaged hull plating. Cot, hundreds of identical notebooks, an autodoc with power cell nearly exhausted. The first entry in every single notebook reads: "Day one. The Endeavour has arrived. Earlier than last time." Later notebooks contain entries about events that haven't happened yet this iteration. One reads: "The tall one with the grey jacket goes to the arch first. Don't let them."

[Ringworld: Research — cross-referencing dates reveals temporal impossibilities; paper shows no age]
The Transit Arch

Standard Ringworld stepping-disc arch — behaving in no standard way. Activates intermittently, no apparent trigger. Produces objects: tools, fragments, once a human hand (disappears before it can be retrieved). Destination display cycles through coordinates that don't resolve. One of the tools bears an engraving matching a player character's equipment.

[Ringworld: Electronics — arch receiving a signal it cannot fully decode, not malfunctioning]
 
[Ringworld: Awareness Hard — engraved tool matches a player character's kit]

Act Two — The Spire

~90 min · Goal: players understand they are the variable

The players discover what the system is, what it is doing, and — crucially — that their presence is the reason it keeps resetting. The emotional pivot: they are not investigators. They are variables.

Locations

Weather Spire — Exterior

~200 metres tall, perfectly circular cross-section. Scrith construction — faint iridescence of nearly-indestructible material. A single entry point at ground level, open. No door. The opening pulses with a light that is not quite any colour the players have a name for. At the base: hundreds of tally marks scratched into the scrith (which should be unscratchable). Marr's work. "I stopped counting," he says.

[Ringworld: Science (Engineering) — power output far beyond a weather control node]
Weather Spire — Control Level

Circular chamber. Walls are read-out surfaces — not screens but surfaces that shift and reconfigure, showing data in real time. In the centre: a raised dais with a single interface hemisphere that responds to biological contact. The interface will not injure anyone, but it displays targeted information drawn from the system's understanding of that individual. What it displays is not always welcome.

[Ringworld: Computer 75% — partial translation reveals iteration count and mass accumulation figures]
 
[Ringworld: Science (Physics) Hard — mass accumulation rate implies self-sustaining singularity within 4–6 hours]
Weather Spire — Log Chamber

Smaller room, passage known only to Marr (or a very fortunate Awareness roll). The system's actual iteration record — not in any language, but a combination of Computer, Linguistics, and Science (Physics) can begin to translate. Three hundred years of logs. Like reading the minutes of a meeting that has been happening for three centuries with no resolution.

[Ringworld: Linguistics + Computer combined — full translation; partial on either alone]

Act Two Clue Trail

C5
Essential · Log Chamber — "Conscious variable introduced. Calculation corrupted. Reset initiated." The players are the reason the system resets; their presence is the unsolvable variable. Fallback: Marr recites this line from memory: "Conscious variable. That's us. That's always been us."
C6
Essential · Control Level — Mass accumulation data on the walls. [Computer] The singularity is becoming real. The system is past the point of simulation.
C7
Optional · Log Chamber — Iteration count: 847. Over three centuries. This is not a new problem.
C8
Optional · Control Level dais — When touched, the interface shows personalised data. The system has already catalogued each player since they crossed the boundary. It has been watching them the whole time.

Act Two Story Hooks

  • Loop tightening: Echo a real table moment — something minor someone said earlier. Not exactly. With a difference. Use it once, hard.
  • Talren over comms: Pak library fragments recovered 80 years ago reference this type of system. The Pak theorised it but never activated it. The Ringworld is doing something the Pak only dreamed of.
  • Marr's near-miss: "In iteration 612, someone found a way to communicate with it directly. I don't know what they said. The loop reset before I could ask."
  • [Ringworld: POW×5] Players near the interface may flash on an alternate iteration — themselves, making a different choice, the system responding differently.
Act Two Fast-Track · Use at 2:45 elapsed The same five seconds of a player speaking replay three times in a row, audible to everyone at the table. Marr says, with zero drama: "It's accelerating. We need to go down. Now." Move immediately.

Act Three — The Core

~30 min · Hard stop at 3:15

This is not a fight. It is a decision made under impossible conditions. The system will accept input. The players must decide what to give it. Do not steer. Do not hint. Let them decide.

The Descent

Circular shaft in the Spire's lowest level, ~3m diameter, with handholds. Gravity fluctuates from 50m down — not dangerously, but players notice their weight changing. During descent, loops manifest visually for the first time: half-visible echoes of themselves descending in slightly different configurations. They do not interact. They are simply there.

[Ringworld: CON×5 — resist mild disorientation from gravity fluctuation]

Marr will not descend. He stays at the top and tells them: "Someone always goes in. No one has come back wrong. But the loop always resets after. I don't know if it's because you fail, or because you succeed."

The Core Chamber

Spherical chamber, ~40m diameter. Scrith walls transparent — through them, the Ringworld's superstructure and the void beyond. At the exact centre: the singularity. Size of a human fist. Warps light around it. Looking directly at it feels like trying to read text that keeps moving.

The system communicates in simplified form — not language but intent, displayed on the walls in shifting mathematical notation. Three statements repeat:

STABILITY REQUIRED
VARIABLES INTERFERE
RESOLUTION PENDING
[Ringworld: Science (Mathematics) Hard — system is asking a question it cannot phrase in terms they can naturally understand]
 
[Ringworld: Awareness Hard — perceives flickers of alternate outcomes at the edge of vision]

The System — Three Forms of Input It Will Accept

Input TypeWhat the Players Must DoRolls Required
Mathematical data Feed the system information that helps it recalculate the mass imbalance. Requires genuine understanding of the problem from Acts One and Two. Computer + Science (Physics), Hard difficulty
Physical action Remove the source of the mass discrepancy. Requires identifying what it actually is — only possible if players assembled all clues C1–C6. Clue-based — no single roll
Withdrawal All conscious variables leave the region completely and remain outside for a calculated period. Marr knows this option. Requires abandoning the ship's orbit position. No roll — pure decision
What the System Cannot Accept Destruction of the singularity. If players attempt to destroy or disrupt the core directly, the system triggers an immediate imperfect reset. Players wake at the Introduction with fragmented memories — they know something has happened, but not what.
[Ringworld: Luck×5 — or lose 1D6 PP from cognitive disruption if caught in partial reset]

Act Three Pressure Points

  • Vorn's comms (hard deadline): "I'm breaking orbit in twenty minutes. With or without you."
  • Marr from the shaft: "Last time someone did nothing, I woke up here again. I don't want to wake up here again."
  • At 3:10 elapsed: The singularity begins audibly resonating — a tone that increases in pitch. Vorn's comms cut out. The loops stop entirely. The system is no longer iterating. It is converging. This forces a decision.
  • Vision flicker: A player sees an alternate outcome — recognisable, making a choice, the walls going dark, then silence. Whether this is good or bad is deliberately unresolvable.

Outcomes

Epilogue ~15 min
Full Success Players provide sufficient data or remove the mass discrepancy source
The system completes a clean iteration. The singularity dissipates. The quiet zone normalises over about an hour. Marr is released from the loop. The Foolish Endeavour receives a single, clear signal: RESOLUTION COMPLETE.

Read aloud: "The tone drops. Not gradually — it simply stops, between one moment and the next. Where the singularity was, there is nothing. Not a void. Just air. When you surface from the shaft, Marr is sitting on the floor, back against the wall, eyes closed. He opens them. 'It's quiet. The right kind of quiet.'"

Partial Success Players withdraw all conscious variables from the region and maintain distance
System completes an iteration, but incompletely. The quiet zone remains. The singularity does not grow further. Marr leaves with the players — but he is not certain the loop is over. The ship leaves safely. The anomaly is listed as contained, unresolved in the survey record.
Failure — Loop Reset Players unable to decide, or attempt to destroy the core
Loop reset triggers. Players wake at the Introduction with fragmented memories — they know something has happened, but not what. Optional: run a compressed Act One with partial memories, ending with a harder choice at the core. The Foolish Endeavour receives a signal. Clear. Precise. Familiar. The voices in the transmission are their own.
Pyrrhic Players allow the system to complete its process without intervention
The singularity becomes self-sustaining. The region is lost — a permanent gravitational anomaly on the Ringworld's inner surface. The Foolish Endeavour breaks orbit barely in time. Marr, who chose to stay at the shaft head, is not seen again. The players leave with everything intact and a discovery that will reshape Known Space cosmology — and one man unaccounted for.
Post-Session Spin-Off Seed

The 848th Iteration

Analysis of Marr's notebooks after the session reveals something no one noticed during play: in notebook 731, there is an entry written in a different hand. Neat, precise, written in a dialect of Interworld Standard that hasn't been in common use for approximately sixty years.

The entry reads: "The system is not the problem. The system is the answer to a different question. Find the question."

It is signed with a symbol that, with the right Research roll, matches the personal insignia of a Pak Protector — one identified in ARM records as having been active in the Ringworld region three centuries ago.

The mass imbalance is not an engineering error. It was placed there deliberately. Someone built an anomaly into the Ringworld, then built a system to keep trying — forever — to resolve it. As if the resolution process itself was the point. As if something needed the Ringworld to keep running this calculation, indefinitely, producing output no one has yet identified.

The players have stopped the loop. They may have also stopped something else.
